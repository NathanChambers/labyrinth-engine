# Irradiance volumes

**Status:** Implemented two-bounce diffuse lighting for authored volumes and the camera-following three-level variable-density clipmap, selectable volume/clipmap/combined modes, and experimental triangle-surface SDF GI with one uniform scene-sized radiance field using equal-area angular segments
**Owner:** `crates/engine` configuration and `crates/renderer` GPU drawing
**Source:** [gi.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gi.rs), [gizmo.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gizmo.rs), [renderer lib.rs](../../crates/renderer/src/lib.rs), [shader.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/shader.wgsl), [sdf_update.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/sdf_update.wgsl), [gi_gizmo.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/gi_gizmo.wgsl), [depth_prepass.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/depth_prepass.wgsl)

## Purpose and boundary

The Primitives, Lighting, Genos, Cornell Box, and Sponza examples select AABB-SDF GI. Authored volumes and clipmaps remain available through the engine API, but these examples do not use them while AABB-SDF is the active GI path. `RuntimeContext::gi_radiance_probe_relocation_enabled` controls whether world-field samples can move away from cell centers; it defaults on, and changing it invalidates the field for a full refresh.

`IrradianceVolumes` owns app-created world-space volume descriptions. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_mode` selects authored volumes, a camera-following irradiance clipmap, both, or experimental AABB-SDF GI. The nested clipmap extents and resolutions come from `RuntimeContext::irradiance_clipmap`; each level snaps its center to that level's probe spacing. The renderer owns the authored-volume/clipmap probe cache, bounded CPU updates, cached surface samples, a coarse visibility grid, and normal-facing probe sampling. AABB-SDF mode bypasses those caches and uses a triangle-surface distance/material field plus one static scene-bounded radiance field for final raster GI. The SDF targets 0.375 m voxel spacing; the radiance field targets 1.5 m probe spacing by default, configurable through `RuntimeContext::gi_radiance_field_cell_size`. Each grid derives its per-axis cell counts by rounding the scene bounds plus horizontal/vertical padding up to whole cells. The resulting grid bounds remain fixed in world space while geometry stays inside them; if geometry moves outside, the grid re-centers with its initialized dimensions and refreshes radiance data. Camera movement does not scroll the grids. The renderer publishes the current field bounds and cell counts through `RuntimeContext::gi_radiance_field_grid` for world-position-to-cell inspection. Raster shading samples the world field instead of tracing a GI ray for every pixel.

## State and invariants

Authored volumes and clipmap probes compute exactly two diffuse bounces. The renderer keeps a direct-only CPU cache separate from the final GPU cache: bounce one evaluates direct lights, emission, and sky at probes; bounce two samples that direct cache at diffuse surface samples, applies their albedo and visibility, and adds the reflected result to the final probe value. Bounce two never reads the final cache, so repeated updates cannot recursively accumulate extra bounces. Both caches use the same bounded probe update schedule and preserve overlapping world-space probes when volume layouts change. The number of bounces is currently fixed at two. The separate AABB-SDF radiance-field path stores direct-hit radiance and computes one additional diffuse bounce in a second GPU pass.

Probe source surfaces use a deterministic, bounded set of triangle samples distributed approximately uniformly over each mesh's world-space surface area. Their per-mesh weights sum to one, so sample density does not change a material's contribution. The visibility grid uses 32 × 64 × 32 cells, with finer vertical spacing so probes can remain close to floors. Probe seeds inside occupied cells search up to three cells along the grid axes for clear positions; a seed with a valid free-space region stays within that region, while an occupied seed selects the candidate in the largest connected free-space region.

Each volume has minimum and maximum world-space bounds and a three-dimensional probe resolution. Three nested clipmap levels are centered on the active camera. Their extents grow as probe densities decrease; each level snaps independently, preserves cached values at overlapping world-space probe positions, and schedules newly exposed probes through the normal bounded refresh budget. The near level covers 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, keeping common floor and wall receivers inside the fine level before the coarse transition begins. Its even vertical resolution places the room-test floor between probe rows at the tested camera height, avoiding a coplanar row at the floor. The shader favors earlier, finer volumes in their interior and cross-fades to later, coarser volumes over two local probe spacings near a volume boundary. If a level has no valid front-facing trilinear support, its blend weight transfers to later volumes. Authored volumes therefore retain priority in combined mode away from their boundaries, while clipmap levels provide smooth overlap and coarser fallback support. Irradiance data uses a read-only storage buffer with a 4,096-probe capacity and four volume descriptors; combined mode reserves one descriptor per active clipmap level. Disabled authored volumes are skipped. Each refreshed probe stores sky visibility in its alpha channel and a weighted dominant incoming direction in a second compact buffer. The irradiance cache stores evaluated probe positions and a validity bit; connected-region labels remain in the CPU visibility grid. The renderer caches samples from static meshes until their transforms or materials change, rebuilds dynamic samples for moving meshes, voxelizes world-space mesh bounds, computes a distance field and connected free-space region labels, and retains those labels for CPU-side probe placement and bounce filtering. Probe locations inside occupied cells are invalidated; probes near geometry can move only to a clear cell in their original region. Directly lit and emissive surface samples use distance-weighted contributions, per-instance sample normalization, and probe-to-surface visibility tests. The shader offsets horizontal receivers by three quarters of local probe spacing along their normal and near-vertical receivers by one quarter. For near-vertical receivers it also shifts the interpolation point upward by one half of vertical probe spacing, keeping wall samples near floor joins off the floor-aligned probe row. The validity bit excludes probes invalidated by geometry from both the color sample and the interpolation normalization. The shader excludes probes whose signed distance from the receiver plane is zero or negative, blends front-side trilinear support, and attenuates probes by their angular alignment with the normal, so nearly perpendicular probes contribute less. Color and directional estimates are normalized by the same angularly weighted support, avoiding a darkening when only a small subset of front-facing probes contribute. Probes behind the surface are excluded, while probes in front remain available across connected-region boundaries. Connected-region labels still constrain probe relocation and CPU-side bounce accumulation. The renderer sphere-traces five axis-aligned rays per refreshed probe. Emissive contributions also add their source direction to the compact directional response. Distance-field rebuilds are throttled while geometry moves, and probe lighting continues on its bounded per-frame schedule. Refreshed values are blended into the cache to hide individual-probe update boundaries. Stale probes near the active camera are prioritized.

Clipmap samples are trilinearly interpolated within each level, then cross-faded across the shared bounds. The transition width accounts for the finer and coarser spacing and is capped by their overlap, avoiding a short band tuned only to the fine grid. Probe-to-surface and local-light-to-surface visibility rays end just outside the sampled surface and use a tight endpoint tolerance derived from the smallest visibility-grid cell dimension, so an obstruction near the target is not automatically treated as visible.

AABB-SDF GI is an experimental two-bounce path with a scene-wide grid of cell-centered radiance probes with optional relocation, separate from the authored-volume and clipmap probe caches. The SDF targets 0.375 m voxels and the radiance field targets 1.5 m probe spacing. Each volume is computed from scene bounds plus 8 m horizontal and 4 m vertical padding, then rounded up independently per axis to a whole number of cells. Camera movement does not move the SDF bounds or rebuild occupancy. The field stays fixed while geometry remains within its initialized bounds; when geometry moves outside them, the grid re-centers with the same cell counts and rebuilds. The renderer builds a BVH over transformed source triangles and finds the closest triangle for each updated SDF voxel. It stores an unsigned distance-to-surface minus half a voxel diagonal, so the zero crossing forms a conservative, roughly one-voxel-thick surface shell instead of filling the mesh's object bounds. Voxels retain the closest triangle's average vertex color multiplied by the material base color and the material emission; PBR texture maps are not represented in this field. Concavities and openings remain empty unless their width is smaller than the shell thickness. The clearance field is bounded to four voxels and built from shell occupancy. `SDF occupied voxels` visualizes those surface cells.

The emission texture alpha channel stores a conservative clearance lower bound, capped at four voxels. A 26-neighbor chessboard distance transform measures distance in voxel layers to the nearest occupied shell cell; multiplying by the smallest voxel size and subtracting the voxel half-diagonal keeps the center value below the distance to occupied voxel volume. Since the grid is static, camera motion never propagates clearance or rebuilds the distance field. The shader subtracts the current sample's offset from its voxel center and uses this value only to skip empty space. Near geometry, grid traversal visits cells and confirms a hit from shell occupancy. Hit positions therefore have voxel-level precision, and normals are gradients of the interpolated unsigned distance, oriented against the incoming ray. Trace termination is explicit: `HIT`, `MAX_STEPS`, `MAX_DISTANCE`, or `GRID_EXIT`. Only a hit evaluates surface lighting; completed maximum-distance and grid-exit traces contribute sky radiance, while step exhaustion remains unresolved and preserves an existing sample or leaves a new one invalid. For direct-light visibility, `MAX_STEPS` remains blocked, while `GRID_EXIT` is visible because the scene-bounded SDF includes padding around all scene geometry. Coarse field rays travel at most 12 m, and direct-light rays stop at their light; each traversal is capped at 96 distance/voxel steps. The source triangle BVH avoids filling concave or hollow mesh bounds; openings narrower than the voxel shell can still close at this resolution. PBR texture maps are not included in the SDF material data.

The static scene-bounded radiance cache is one uniform world-space field, centered on active scene geometry bounds and fixed in world space while geometry stays within the current bounds. Its per-axis counts come from the padded scene extents divided by the target spacing, rounded up. The default target is 1.5 m and can be configured from 0.25–8 m. The renderer caps the field at 16,384 cells; if the padded grid would exceed that limit, it raises the effective spacing until the field fits. The effective spacing is visible in the published grid bounds and resolution. Each cell stores up to 64 equal-solid-angle regions, partitioned into 2 × 4, 4 × 8, or 8 × 8 bands and sectors. The default is 32 regions. The inspector can select 8, 32, or 64 regions; changing resolution clears and refreshes the field. Each region owns its direct-radiance mean, resolved sample count, attempt count, and last-update frame. A bounded 1,024-ray work budget schedules at most 16 cells per frame, with up to 64 jittered rays per selected cell. Ray pairs sample opposite ends of the round-robin region order. Resolved samples update only their region; unresolved rays advance that region’s attempt sequence but do not affect its mean. A second GPU pass retraces each resolved scheduled region and writes a separate final-radiance cache: direct-hit radiance plus surface albedo times direct-cache irradiance sampled at the hit. The pass provides one additional diffuse bounce; it does not recursively read its own output or propagate later bounces. Rays point from each probe toward their hit surface, so the receiver cosine weights that same angular direction. Shading cosine-weights final region radiance over the equal-area partition and blends toward sky radiance in proportion to valid spatial and directional support, so an uninitialized or under-sampled field fades in instead of returning black GI. The selected cell exposes the first eight direct-pass traces from its latest update. The renderer stores a fixed 64-slot direct angular layout per cell (2 KiB) and a second 16-byte-per-slot radiance buffer, plus compact coverage summaries, probe state, and up to eight trace records. This is a two-bounce probe cache, not Radiance Cascades.

The current diagnostic and final field shading sample at `P_surface + N * 0.1 m`. Final field shading uses trilinear spatial weights for the surrounding cells, applies a smooth same-side weight using the receiver normal and each relocated probe position, and cosine-weights each valid cell's equal-area angular-region radiance by the receiver normal. The side weight is smoothstep over a 0.2 m range centered on the offset shading point. It reduces contributions from probes behind the receiver plane, but does not verify line of sight through arbitrary geometry. Final gather rejects invalid probes and probes with negligible same-side weight before scanning their angular regions, and reuses the loaded probe record for the scan. Relocation is enabled by default and moves nearby probes to clear ray origins; disabling it leaves probes at cell centers and marks those without clearance invalid. Invalid probes are excluded from interpolation independently of same-side weighting. The surface SDF is unsigned, so its interpolated gradient points away from the nearest shell on either side; trace hits orient that gradient against the incoming ray, making GI surface lighting two-sided.

Each angular segment is an equal-area estimator rather than a broad directional lobe. A running cursor advances by the number of ray pairs in an update, with each pair sampling opposite ends of the region order. Initial population and hard field invalidation queue cell-history resets; while resets remain, the scheduler spends its 1,024-ray budget on up to 16 reset cells per frame and defers other work. It then schedules lighting-priority and camera-focus cells, ordinary dirty updates, and round-robin refinement when budget remains. Geometry, light, and sky changes queue history-preserving requests that coalesce while pending; camera-cell changes replace the camera-focus queue with nearby cells. Repeated changes therefore do not continually re-add cells already processed in the current refresh. Each region's running mean uses at most 16 resolved samples; further updates use a fixed 1/16 blend so old radiance decays over time. With refinement disabled, only queued work runs. Freeze pauses the update pass while retaining queued work. A compact GPU summary is copied asynchronously every 30 rendered frames and reports field-wide region coverage (sampled once, at least twice, and attempted), relocation-valid cells, and cells with any coverage. The selected-cell debug view reads back the first eight traces from its latest update and all 64 region records. The selected-cell sphere is opaque grayscale: a resolved sample turns its region white, then it fades exponentially toward black with a 180-frame time constant. Unsampled and unresolved regions stay black, and failed traces do not refresh a region’s age.

The former screen-space surface-probe cache, compute updates, raster gather, and gizmo rendering have been removed. AABB-SDF raster GI now uses only the coarse world-radiance field.

Radiance-field rays that hit geometry resolve with the hit's direct and emissive radiance. Rays that reach their maximum distance or leave the padded SDF bounds resolve with configured sky radiance, including zero radiance when sky lighting is disabled. These escaped rays count as covered angular samples; rays that exhaust the step limit remain unresolved and do not increase coverage.

The lighting inspector selects a cell in the single radiance field and shows the first eight traces from its latest update, labeled R0 through R7; all paths appear in the gizmo while the selection controls the detailed readout. Pick selects the nearest radiance cell to a clicked mesh surface; W/S, A/D, and R/F move one field cell along Z, X, and Y. The inspector reads field resolution and world bounds from the renderer and displays field size, per-cell spacing, selected cell center, and asynchronous field-wide coverage and scheduling stats. Geometry mode colors paths by hit/miss, shows the endpoint, hit normal, termination reason, and travelled distance. Radiance mode uses a neutral path, tone-mapped trace-time radiance at hits, a blue normal, and an orange marker for non-hit traces. It displays raw RGB plus direct, emission, and sky contributions before direct-cache accumulation, the currently stored direct-region RGB, signed raw-minus-stored RGB difference, and age in field-update frames. It also shows the ray direction, accumulated sample count for the corresponding angular region, and blend factor. The renderer reads the stored value from the direct cache and retains the last update frame beside each raw trace record; this diagnostic is asynchronous and refreshes with each cell update. Each resolved ray blends into its region using a sample history capped at 16. The inspector reports hit position and normal from the same trace record. Completed `MAX_DISTANCE` and `GRID_EXIT` traces receive sky radiance; `MAX_STEPS` remains unresolved and reports zero sky. A cell outline highlights the selected field cell. Other gizmo categories, including SDF occupancy boxes, are hidden in Radiance mode unless `Show other gizmos` is enabled. `SDF occupied voxels` separately draws translucent boxes for occupied voxels that border empty cells, showing the field boundary without drawing every interior voxel.

## Public contract

The default levels cover 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, 64 × 48 × 64 at 8 × 5 × 8 probes, and 128 × 96 × 128 at 6 × 4 × 6 probes. The near level has tighter vertical spacing and a larger horizontal footprint; probe density falls at each larger scale. The three levels use 2,830 probes total; combined with the 1,008-probe room-test volume they fit within the 4,096-probe capacity and four descriptors.

Applications call `context.irradiance_volumes.add(IrradianceVolumeDesc { ... })` during setup and retain the returned `IrradianceVolumeId` when they need to mutate the description. They can set `context.irradiance_mode` to `Volumes`, `Clipmap`, `Combined`, or experimental `AabbSdf`, configure irradiance clipmap levels through `context.irradiance_clipmap`, and set the target world-space probe spacing in meters through `context.gi_radiance_field_cell_size` before renderer initialization. Values are clamped to 0.25–8 m; the renderer may increase effective spacing to keep the padded world field within its 16,384-cell cap. AABB-SDF mode bypasses the authored-volume and clipmap caches; field rays evaluate the nearest triangle's stored base material and emission, direct lights, and sky. `RuntimeContext::gi_trace_diagnostics` contains up to eight asynchronously read back trace results and matching stored angular-region samples for the selected cell, captured before cache interpolation; each record includes raw and stored RGB, signed difference, last-update age, sampled ray direction, region resolved/attempted sample counts, and blend factor. `RuntimeContext::gizmos_enabled` is the master switch for overlays and defaults to off; the individual normal, light, global GI, trace and SDF occupancy toggles also default to off. `normal_gizmos_enabled`, `light_gizmos_enabled`, and `gi_global_gizmos_enabled` select mesh normals, light markers, volume/clipmap markers, and the world radiance field. `gi_trace_gizmos_enabled` displays the latest scheduled rays from the cell selected by `gi_selected_radiance_cell`; in the Lighting example, opening the Selected GI cell rays popout enables these traces and master gizmo visibility until it closes. The popout contains cell selection, raw/stored radiance values, signed difference, update age, sampling direction and accumulation diagnostics, cache freeze, refinement, and a one-shot scene GPU timing sample button. `sdf_occupancy_gizmos_enabled` draws SDF boundary voxels. `freeze_gi_radiance` remains a diagnostic switch for app or capture control: it pauses coarse world-field refresh while retaining current field contents. The Lighting GI inspector exposes a cache-freeze control for validation. Pending scripted captures suppress all overlays. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_update_budget` controls the maximum number of probes refreshed per frame in probe modes. `RuntimeContext::sky_lighting` controls sky radiance for probe updates and SDF misses. `RuntimeContext::performance_stats` exposes probe update time and counts plus SDF build/upload time, updated voxel count, radiance-field compute time, scene-pass GPU time, observed region coverage once and twice, attempted regions, relocation-valid cells, cells with coverage, queued cells, scheduled cells, rays scheduled, active angular-region count, and whether a coverage readback is available. Coverage is an asynchronous full-field summary updated every 30 rendered frames and counts actual sampled regions. `RuntimeContext::request_gpu_timing_sample` is a one-shot engine request consumed after rendering; it blocks for one timestamp readback. GPU time is also refreshed by scripted timestamp reports. `context.gizmos` exposes line, wire-box, ring, dot, and sampled mesh-normal helpers for additional diagnostics.

The renderer returns up to eight asynchronous trace diagnostics for the selected field cell, updated with each cell sample. Each record includes trace-time radiance components, termination reason, travelled distance, hit position, and hit normal when a hit occurs. `RuntimeContext::gi_trace_debug_mode` selects `Geometry` or `Radiance`. The lighting inspector also displays the selected cell’s 8, 32, or 64 angular region estimates as paged RGB/sample rows and an opaque freshness sphere: resolved regions turn white and fade toward black over time, while unsampled or unresolved regions are black. The lighting inspector owns these controls as a projection of `RuntimeContext` state. Radiance mode uses a neutral ray line, radiance-colored cross dot at hits, blue normal, and orange miss marker; Geometry mode retains hit/miss path colors and termination/distance markers. `RenderDebugMode::GiSupport` colors receiving surfaces from single-field support: red shows the fraction of valid cells, green the remaining spatial weight, and blue angular support; magenta means no usable samples. Gray means the world radiance field is unavailable. Opening Radiance mode suppresses other gizmo categories by default; the inspector's Show other gizmos toggle is the explicit override.

The shared GI Surface Inspector requests the clicked viewport pixel and evaluates it in a renderer compute pass after the depth prepass. The compute pass reconstructs world position from depth and the camera inverse view-projection matrix, reads the world normal, and calls the same interpolation-candidate and angular-region helpers as final raster GI. Both paths use each candidate's relocated probe position to apply a soft same-side weight: the dot product of the receiver normal and the vector from the offset shading point to the relocated probe is mapped through a 0.2 m smooth transition. This reduces the weight of probes behind the receiving surface, then normalizes the remaining weights. It is a receiver-plane heuristic, not a segment visibility test, so it does not guarantee separation around corners or for ambiguous thin geometry.


## Lifecycle

The engine creates the volume set, clipmap configuration, and default radiance-field cell spacing before application setup. Applications add or mutate authored volumes, select a GI mode, and may override the target radiance probe spacing during setup. Renderer initialization snapshots that spacing and the scene bounds, adds padding, then rounds each axis up to a whole number of cells. When geometry moves within the initialized grid bounds, the renderer updates affected SDF surface voxels and clearance regions while keeping both grids anchored. If geometry moves outside those bounds, the renderer re-centers each grid with its initialized dimensions, clears re-anchored radiance data, and schedules fresh samples. Each redraw passes the active camera, scene instances, and lights to the renderer. In AABB-SDF mode the renderer rebuilds the triangle BVH as needed and updates surface-distance voxels only in dirty regions; radiance gathering stays within a 1,024-ray per-frame ceiling and up to 32 cell invocations. At the default 32 angular regions, initial and dirty updates take one sample per region, so the full ray budget can cover up to 32 cells; local-light priority and later refinement use up to 64 rays per cell. Moving point and spot lights prioritize cells within their affected range; if a priority cell is awaiting its first reset, it preempts that queue and consumes the reset once. Camera-focused cells use the same preemption rule. Remaining reset cells use the budget before ordinary dirty updates and round-robin refinement. Directional-light changes request one coalesced whole-field sweep after any initial reset pass, and geometry changes request a coalesced sweep after reset work. Repeated changes while a sweep is pending do not rebuild or reorder the whole queue. Camera movement changes neither grid bounds nor cell ownership; crossing a field-cell boundary queues a nearby focus neighborhood. Freeze skips the field update pass without consuming queued refresh work. Optional diagnostics draw the first eight traces from the selected field cell's latest update and the boundary shell voxels. Initial population and hard invalidation reset old sample history as cells are serviced; in-bounds geometry, light, and sky changes queue history-preserving updates. The room-test point light can be moved with I/J/K/L/U/O, while WASD moves the camera. The field and its diagnostic resources are released with the renderer.

## Verification and open questions

Each radiance cell has a compact relocation record containing the effective ray origin and a status value. The compute path writes this record for every refreshed cell. With relocation disabled, it stores the cell center and marks cells without clearance invalid; when enabled, it can store a moved ray origin. Field interpolation excludes records with a non-positive status, including after geometry changes clear the records and before refresh completes. The relocation state is for probe validity and ray origin ownership; the soft same-side factor is evaluated per receiving surface and is not stored in the relocation record.

The equal-area angular field improves capture of small bright directions by keeping samples isolated in smaller regions. The 32-region default balances angular detail with gradual convergence; 8 regions trades detail for faster coverage, while 64 regions can resolve finer patches but needs more refinement frames to cover and stabilize the sphere. The field still inherits finite voxel thickness, missing PBR texture detail, and low spatial resolution. Final shading uses trilinear interpolation among valid cells, receiver-plane same-side weighting, and cosine-weighted integration over the angular regions. It does not implement Radiance Cascade interval hierarchy, recursive multi-bounce transport beyond the one additional diffuse bounce, or motion reprojection.

Verification: `cargo test -p renderer radiance_field_tests` passes all 11 focused tests, and `cargo check -p renderer -p genos` compiles both crates. A Vulkan Genos capture at 10.7 hours with sky and point lights disabled reached 100% first-sample and twice-sampled angular coverage, 16 updated cells, and 1,024 scheduled direct rays. The GI-only view shows a broad ceiling fill, but the floor and far walls remain very dim after coverage completes. A temporary third-pass capture at full coverage produced only a modest ceiling change and still left the floor dark, so an additional bounce was not retained. The direct and second-bounce timing varied substantially between captures; low-end GPU performance remains unverified.

More bounces require another cache iteration that reads the previous bounce output, plus a separate output buffer or ping-pong state to avoid read/write races. The full-coverage capture indicates that direct-to-indirect hit sampling or spatial convergence needs attention before adding bounce cost. The two-pass and experimental three-pass captures are under `captures/genos-sun-10-7-angular-forward/` and `captures/genos-sun-10-7-third-bounce-forward/`.
