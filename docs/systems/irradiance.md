# Irradiance volumes

**Status:** Implemented authored volumes and a camera-following three-level variable-density clipmap, selectable volume/clipmap/combined modes, and an experimental AABB-SDF hybrid with coarse world radiance and persistent surface probes
**Owner:** `crates/engine` configuration and `crates/renderer` GPU drawing
**Source:** [gi.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gi.rs), [gizmo.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gizmo.rs), [renderer lib.rs](../../crates/renderer/src/lib.rs), [shader.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/shader.wgsl), [depth_prepass.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/depth_prepass.wgsl)

## Purpose and boundary

`IrradianceVolumes` owns app-created world-space volume descriptions. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_mode` selects authored volumes, a camera-following irradiance clipmap, both, or experimental AABB-SDF GI. The nested clipmap extents and resolutions come from `RuntimeContext::irradiance_clipmap`; each level snaps its center to that level's probe spacing. The renderer owns a persistent probe cache, bounded CPU updates, cached surface samples, a coarse visibility grid, and normal-facing probe sampling. AABB-SDF mode bypasses probe volumes and their update path. It uses a camera-centered AABB distance/material field for visibility, a coarse world radiance field for off-screen fallback, and fixed-lattice surface probes for visible local detail. Raster shading samples these caches instead of tracing a GI ray for every pixel.

## State and invariants

Each volume has minimum and maximum world-space bounds and a three-dimensional probe resolution. Three nested clipmap levels are centered on the active camera. Their extents grow as probe densities decrease; each level snaps independently, preserves cached values at overlapping world-space probe positions, and schedules newly exposed probes through the normal bounded refresh budget. The near level covers 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, keeping common floor and wall receivers inside the fine level before the coarse transition begins. Its even vertical resolution places the room-test floor between probe rows at the tested camera height, avoiding a coplanar row at the floor. The shader favors earlier, finer volumes in their interior and cross-fades to later, coarser volumes over two local probe spacings near a volume boundary. If a level has no valid front-facing trilinear support, its blend weight transfers to later volumes. Authored volumes therefore retain priority in combined mode away from their boundaries, while clipmap levels provide smooth overlap and coarser fallback support. Irradiance data uses a read-only storage buffer with a 4,096-probe capacity and four volume descriptors; combined mode reserves one descriptor per active clipmap level. Disabled authored volumes are skipped. Each refreshed probe stores sky visibility in its alpha channel and a weighted dominant incoming direction in a second compact buffer. The irradiance cache stores evaluated probe positions and a validity bit; connected-region labels remain in the CPU visibility grid. The renderer caches samples from static meshes until their transforms or materials change, rebuilds dynamic samples for moving meshes, voxelizes world-space mesh bounds, computes a distance field and connected free-space region labels, and retains those labels for CPU-side probe placement and bounce filtering. Probe locations inside occupied cells are invalidated; probes near geometry can move only to a clear cell in their original region. Directly lit and emissive surface samples use distance-weighted contributions, per-instance sample normalization, and probe-to-surface visibility tests. The shader offsets horizontal receivers by three quarters of local probe spacing along their normal and near-vertical receivers by one quarter. For near-vertical receivers it also shifts the interpolation point upward by one half of vertical probe spacing, keeping wall samples near floor joins off the floor-aligned probe row. The validity bit excludes probes invalidated by geometry from both the color sample and the interpolation normalization. The shader excludes probes whose signed distance from the receiver plane is zero or negative, blends front-side trilinear support, and attenuates probes by their angular alignment with the normal, so nearly perpendicular probes contribute less. Color and directional estimates are normalized by the same angularly weighted support, avoiding a darkening when only a small subset of front-facing probes contribute. Probes behind the surface are excluded, while probes in front remain available across connected-region boundaries. Connected-region labels still constrain probe relocation and CPU-side bounce accumulation. The renderer sphere-traces five axis-aligned rays per refreshed probe. Emissive contributions also add their source direction to the compact directional response. Distance-field rebuilds are throttled while geometry moves, and probe lighting continues on its bounded per-frame schedule. Refreshed values are blended into the cache to hide individual-probe update boundaries. Stale probes near the active camera are prioritized.

Clipmap samples are trilinearly interpolated within each level, then cross-faded across the shared bounds. The transition width accounts for the finer and coarser spacing and is capped by their overlap, avoiding a short band tuned only to the fine grid. Probe-to-surface and local-light-to-surface visibility rays end just outside the sampled surface and use a tight endpoint tolerance derived from the smallest visibility-grid cell dimension, so an obstruction near the target is not automatically treated as visible.

AABB-SDF GI is an experimental probe-free, single-bounce path. Its 64 × 32 × 64 clipmap covers 32 × 16 × 32 world units with 0.5 m voxels. The horizontal bounds follow the camera center; vertically, 87.5% of the range lies below the camera to keep floors and nearby receivers inside the field when the camera is elevated. The renderer owns a toroidal texture offset: camera movement by less than one voxel leaves the grid fixed, and smaller-than-volume shifts rebuild only the exposed occupancy slabs. After a slab update, the renderer recomputes clearance for the complete logical grid because a newly occupied cell can change safe travel distances elsewhere. A geometry change or shift at least as large as the field causes a full rebuild. Each voxel stores the closest proxy's base albedo, emission, and signed distance. Before sampling, each proxy AABB is expanded by half a voxel on each axis, so voxel cells intersecting it become occupied; this preserves thin walls at the cost of up to one voxel of added thickness. `SDF occupied voxels` visualizes those occupied boundary cells.

The emission texture alpha channel stores a conservative clearance lower bound. A two-pass 26-neighbor chessboard distance transform measures distance in voxel layers to the nearest occupied cell; multiplying by the smallest voxel size and subtracting the voxel half-diagonal keeps the center value below the distance to occupied voxel volume. The shader subtracts the current sample's offset from its voxel center and uses this value only to skip empty space. Near geometry, grid traversal visits cells and confirms a hit from occupancy. Hit positions therefore have voxel-level precision, and normals remain gradients of the interpolated signed field. Trace termination is explicit: `HIT`, `MAX_STEPS`, `MAX_DISTANCE`, or `GRID_EXIT`. Only a hit evaluates proxy lighting, and only a completed maximum-distance trace contributes sky radiance. Step exhaustion and leaving the clipmap remain unresolved: the coarse cache preserves an existing sample or leaves a new one invalid, and surface probes exclude unresolved rays. Direct-light visibility treats unresolved traversal as blocked. Coarse field rays travel at most 12 m, surface-probe rays 8 m, and direct-light rays stop at their light; each traversal is capped at 96 distance/voxel steps. The SDF represents per-instance AABBs, not source meshes: a concave, hollow, L-shaped, or thinly detailed mesh can become a solid box and create false occlusion. Conservative voxelization favors coverage over exact thickness, and per-geometry SDF resolution or a local high-resolution representation is not implemented.

The camera-centered radiance cache has two independently scrolling levels with 8 × 4 × 8 cells and six directional lobes per cell. The near level covers 16 × 8 × 16 m at 2 m spacing; the far level covers 32 × 16 × 32 m at 4 m spacing. 87.5% of each level's vertical range extends below the camera. Each level refreshes all cells on initialization, an explicit GI mode reset, or a camera shift at least as large as the level. Newly exposed cells refresh immediately. Geometry, light, and sky changes schedule a one-thirty-second rotating refresh for the next 32 frames; unchanged fields stop refreshing after that window. Toroidal indexing preserves radiance for overlapping world cells as the camera moves. Updated values blend 25% toward the new sample during this bounded refresh window. `RuntimeContext::freeze_gi_radiance` pauses scheduled and dirty-cell refreshes while retaining the current field contents; the lighting inspector no longer exposes a control for it. Radiance cells whose centers lie inside SDF solids are invalidated rather than storing lighting from inside geometry. Raster shading trilinearly samples valid cells, estimates diffuse response from the cosine-weighted directional lobes, and applies receiver-to-cell SDF visibility as a soft confidence. It normalizes the result by the same visibility-weighted interpolation support, reducing discontinuous per-cell visibility footprints in the reconstructed lighting. Near-field samples dominate in the interior and cross-fade to the far field over the last 2 m. The two directional caches use about 48 KiB in `vec4<f32>` storage. This is a spatially nested cache, not Radiance Cascades: it has no interval hierarchy or higher-order bounce transport. It can still inherit proxy-SDF errors and angular banding, and per-cell SDF visibility checks add fragment cost. `RendererPerformanceStats` reports SDF CPU build time, upload preparation/enqueue time, rebuilt voxel count, and the radiance-field compute GPU time; the legacy `gi_history_valid_percent` statistic now reports field availability after a completed update. GPU pass timings refresh when the timestamp report is read.

The hybrid path also owns a persistent surface cache. The depth prepass writes geometric normals to an `Rgba16Float` target and per-instance IDs to an `R32Uint` target. The current renderer uses its unjittered camera projection for these samples. A compute grid evaluates one candidate per 16 × 16 tile and keeps only tiles on a deterministic 32 × 32 screen lattice; edge refinement is disabled. A deterministic budget selects up to 3,000 visible base probes per frame, distributing selection evenly across the lattice at resolutions where it exceeds that limit. Each selected tile maps directly to a unique cache slot by its lattice index, including under budget subsampling, so GPU workgroup order cannot change slot ownership or the stable ray-sampling seed. A slot retains its radiance only while its surface ID, geometric normal, and world position remain compatible; otherwise it is reinitialized. The gizmo pass shows probes selected this frame, with their normal segments colored by radiance. Anchors use the exact sampled surface position and geometric normal; rays start 8 cm along the geometric normal. A probe keeps the world-space anchor associated with its stored radiance until it traces again. Movement beyond one quarter SDF voxel forces an immediate trace and replaces the old value; smaller movement reduces the probe's confidence without dragging its anchor. The stable slot index seeds a fixed sampling rotation. New probes trace immediately and stable base probes update every 16 frames. Reused radiance blends at 0.65 for base probes, 0.8 for priority probes, and 0.9 for high-priority probes; a moved anchor replaces its old sample. Slots not selected for up to 240 frames remain available before eviction. Each updated probe traces four diffuse SDF rays. A hit combines proxy direct/emissive lighting with the coarse radiance field; a miss uses sky radiance. Full-resolution shading gathers candidates from a fixed 5 × 5 neighborhood of the screen lattice, then blends up to the 12 nearest compatible probes from the receiver's surface ID with broad tangent-distance falloff and soft plane and normal weights. This fixed traversal removes frame-varying linked-list order from probe selection. For screen-probe visibility, both ray endpoints are offset along the receiver normal so the visibility segment does not enter the receiving surface; the coarse-field visibility check retains its cell-center endpoint. The coarse near/far field remains the fallback for off-screen and uncovered surfaces. The SDF still represents instance AABBs, and the per-pixel candidate gather plus visibility checks need profiling on larger scenes.

Visible surface candidates use deterministic tile-index selection and deterministic cache-slot ownership on the fixed 32 × 32 base lattice, so a fixed depth/normal/ID buffer selects the same tiles and ray seeds every frame even when GPU workgroup scheduling changes. Raster shading searches a fixed 5 × 5 screen-lattice neighborhood, avoiding a concurrent spatial-hash chain. Edge refinement is currently disabled. The lighting inspector's `Selected GI cell rays` overlay displays only the six directional rays for one selected near or far radiance-field cell. X/Y/Z sliders select its coordinate in the 8 × 4 × 8 field. Ray segments show geometry status: hit is green, miss is orange, and invalid or unresolved results are red. Endpoint marker rings show lighting kind, termination reason, and the last sampled voxel distance; the ray segment length shows total travelled distance. Termination colors distinguish hit, maximum steps, maximum travel distance, and clipmap exit. A cell outline highlights the selected field cell. `SDF occupied voxels` separately draws translucent boxes for occupied voxels that border empty cells, showing the field boundary without drawing every interior voxel. The 3,000-probe limit evenly subsamples the surface-probe lattice only when it exceeds the visible probe budget.

## Public contract

The default levels cover 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, 64 × 48 × 64 at 8 × 5 × 8 probes, and 128 × 96 × 128 at 6 × 4 × 6 probes. The near level has tighter vertical spacing and a larger horizontal footprint; probe density falls at each larger scale. The three levels use 2,830 probes total; combined with the 1,008-probe room-test volume they fit within the 4,096-probe capacity and four descriptors.

Applications call `context.irradiance_volumes.add(IrradianceVolumeDesc { ... })` during setup and retain the returned `IrradianceVolumeId` when they need to mutate the description. They can set `context.irradiance_mode` to `Volumes`, `Clipmap`, `Combined`, or experimental `AabbSdf` and configure irradiance clipmap levels through `context.irradiance_clipmap`. AABB-SDF mode bypasses probe volumes; field rays evaluate proxy material, direct lights, emission, and sky. `RuntimeContext::gizmos_enabled` is the master switch for overlays and defaults to off; the individual normal, light, global GI, screen probe, trace, and SDF occupancy toggles also default to off. `normal_gizmos_enabled`, `light_gizmos_enabled`, and `gi_global_gizmos_enabled` select mesh normals, light markers, volume/clipmap markers, and the coarse world radiance field; `gi_gizmos_enabled` selects visible screen-driven surface probes. `gi_trace_gizmos_enabled` displays six traces from the cell selected by `gi_selected_radiance_cell` and `gi_selected_radiance_field_far`; `sdf_occupancy_gizmos_enabled` draws SDF boundary voxels. `freeze_gi_radiance` remains a diagnostic switch for app or capture control: existing screen probes retain their current anchors and radiance, and coarse world-field refresh stops; newly created screen probes still take their initial sample. The lighting inspector does not expose a freeze control. Pending scripted captures suppress all overlays. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_update_budget` controls the maximum number of probes refreshed per frame in probe modes. `RuntimeContext::sky_lighting` controls sky radiance for probe updates and SDF misses. `RuntimeContext::performance_stats` exposes probe update time and counts plus SDF build/upload time, updated voxel count, radiance-field compute time, and field availability (retained under the legacy `gi_history_valid_percent` metric name). GPU time is refreshed when the debug-capture timestamp report is requested. `context.gizmos` exposes line, wire-box, ring, dot, and sampled mesh-normal helpers for additional diagnostics.

## Lifecycle

The engine creates the volume set and clipmap configuration before application setup. Applications add or mutate authored volumes and select a GI mode during setup or update. Each redraw passes the active camera, scene instances, and lights to the renderer. In AABB-SDF mode the renderer uploads exposed SDF slabs, renders current depth and normals, updates coarse radiance, refreshes visible surface probes, ages stale entries, and shades the scene using a fixed lattice neighborhood gather. Optional diagnostics draw the six traces from the selected near/far radiance-field cell and the boundary voxels whose signed distance marks them occupied. Resize recreates screen-sized targets and updates the screen sampling grid without discarding probe-cache allocations. Geometry, light, sky, or mode changes request a full coarse-field refresh. Both caches and their diagnostic resources are released with the renderer.

## Verification and open questions

The twelve directional radiance lobes and finer near level reduce spatial and angular banding, but cannot remove errors from per-instance AABB proxies. Conservative voxelization covers geometry that falls between SDF samples by expanding proxy bounds by half a voxel per axis; this increases apparent thickness and can add shadowing around thin walls. Receiver-to-cell SDF confidence weighting reduces interpolation across walls without hard cell rejection, though it inherits the SDF's coarse geometry. A fixed tile-to-slot mapping and fixed-order 5 × 5 reconstruction gather remove GPU atomic scheduling from screen-probe identity and selection. Static-scene visual stability still needs confirmation in the user's room-test view; the fixed gather may retain spatial softness from the sparse lattice. This prototype does not implement Radiance Cascade interval hierarchy, multi-bounce transport, or motion reprojection. The separate probe modes still use CPU evaluation and retain their 4,096-probe and four-descriptor limits.

Verification: targeted renderer test `conservative_voxel_field_covers_floors_walls_corners_and_thin_geometry_after_scroll`, `cargo fmt --check --package renderer --package lighting`, `cargo check -p lighting --offline`, and `cargo run -p lighting --offline -- --room-test` completed. The room test presented its first frame without wgpu shader or bind-group validation errors. The test checks occupied voxels, verifies a clearance lower bound against proxy distance, and repeats occupancy checks after scrolling; visual ray behavior in the room-test scene still needs inspection.
