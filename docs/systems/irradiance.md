# Irradiance volumes

**Status:** Implemented authored volumes and a camera-following three-level variable-density clipmap, selectable volume/clipmap/combined modes, and experimental AABB-SDF GI with one uniform scene-sized world radiance field; screen-space probes are retained but disabled by default
**Owner:** `crates/engine` configuration and `crates/renderer` GPU drawing
**Source:** [gi.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gi.rs), [gizmo.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gizmo.rs), [renderer lib.rs](../../crates/renderer/src/lib.rs), [shader.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/shader.wgsl), [gi_gizmo.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/gi_gizmo.wgsl), [depth_prepass.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/depth_prepass.wgsl)

## Purpose and boundary

`IrradianceVolumes` owns app-created world-space volume descriptions. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_mode` selects authored volumes, a camera-following irradiance clipmap, both, or experimental AABB-SDF GI. The nested clipmap extents and resolutions come from `RuntimeContext::irradiance_clipmap`; each level snaps its center to that level's probe spacing. The renderer owns the authored-probe cache, bounded CPU updates, cached surface samples, a coarse visibility grid, and normal-facing probe sampling. AABB-SDF mode bypasses probe volumes and their update path. It uses a static AABB distance/material field and one static scene-bounded radiance field for final raster GI. The field resolution defaults to 32 × 8 × 32 cells and applications can override it through `RuntimeContext::gi_radiance_field_resolution` during setup. Its extent is at least the SDF extent and expands as needed to cover active scene geometry bounds plus padding. The configured cell counts stay fixed after initialization, so spacing can increase when the field expands. Geometry-driven bounds changes re-center the grids and clear radiance data before refreshing; camera movement does not scroll them. The renderer publishes the current field bounds and resolution through `RuntimeContext::gi_radiance_field_grid` for world-position-to-cell inspection. The retained fixed-lattice surface-probe cache is disabled by default and is not sampled by final raster shading. Raster shading samples the world field instead of tracing a GI ray for every pixel.

## State and invariants

Each volume has minimum and maximum world-space bounds and a three-dimensional probe resolution. Three nested clipmap levels are centered on the active camera. Their extents grow as probe densities decrease; each level snaps independently, preserves cached values at overlapping world-space probe positions, and schedules newly exposed probes through the normal bounded refresh budget. The near level covers 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, keeping common floor and wall receivers inside the fine level before the coarse transition begins. Its even vertical resolution places the room-test floor between probe rows at the tested camera height, avoiding a coplanar row at the floor. The shader favors earlier, finer volumes in their interior and cross-fades to later, coarser volumes over two local probe spacings near a volume boundary. If a level has no valid front-facing trilinear support, its blend weight transfers to later volumes. Authored volumes therefore retain priority in combined mode away from their boundaries, while clipmap levels provide smooth overlap and coarser fallback support. Irradiance data uses a read-only storage buffer with a 4,096-probe capacity and four volume descriptors; combined mode reserves one descriptor per active clipmap level. Disabled authored volumes are skipped. Each refreshed probe stores sky visibility in its alpha channel and a weighted dominant incoming direction in a second compact buffer. The irradiance cache stores evaluated probe positions and a validity bit; connected-region labels remain in the CPU visibility grid. The renderer caches samples from static meshes until their transforms or materials change, rebuilds dynamic samples for moving meshes, voxelizes world-space mesh bounds, computes a distance field and connected free-space region labels, and retains those labels for CPU-side probe placement and bounce filtering. Probe locations inside occupied cells are invalidated; probes near geometry can move only to a clear cell in their original region. Directly lit and emissive surface samples use distance-weighted contributions, per-instance sample normalization, and probe-to-surface visibility tests. The shader offsets horizontal receivers by three quarters of local probe spacing along their normal and near-vertical receivers by one quarter. For near-vertical receivers it also shifts the interpolation point upward by one half of vertical probe spacing, keeping wall samples near floor joins off the floor-aligned probe row. The validity bit excludes probes invalidated by geometry from both the color sample and the interpolation normalization. The shader excludes probes whose signed distance from the receiver plane is zero or negative, blends front-side trilinear support, and attenuates probes by their angular alignment with the normal, so nearly perpendicular probes contribute less. Color and directional estimates are normalized by the same angularly weighted support, avoiding a darkening when only a small subset of front-facing probes contribute. Probes behind the surface are excluded, while probes in front remain available across connected-region boundaries. Connected-region labels still constrain probe relocation and CPU-side bounce accumulation. The renderer sphere-traces five axis-aligned rays per refreshed probe. Emissive contributions also add their source direction to the compact directional response. Distance-field rebuilds are throttled while geometry moves, and probe lighting continues on its bounded per-frame schedule. Refreshed values are blended into the cache to hide individual-probe update boundaries. Stale probes near the active camera are prioritized.

Clipmap samples are trilinearly interpolated within each level, then cross-faded across the shared bounds. The transition width accounts for the finer and coarser spacing and is capped by their overlap, avoiding a short band tuned only to the fine grid. Probe-to-surface and local-light-to-surface visibility rays end just outside the sampled surface and use a tight endpoint tolerance derived from the smallest visibility-grid cell dimension, so an obstruction near the target is not automatically treated as visible.

AABB-SDF GI is an experimental probe-free, single-bounce path. Its 128 × 32 × 128 grid defaults to 64 × 16 × 64 world units and expands per axis to cover larger scene bounds plus 8 m horizontal and 4 m vertical padding. The room-test remains at the default extent, with 16 m horizontal padding around its 32 m floor and outer walls. The SDF voxel count stays fixed, so larger scenes have coarser voxels. Camera movement does not move the SDF bounds or rebuild occupancy. When scene geometry bounds change, the field re-centers to the new bounds and rebuilds in full; geometry is no longer clipped just because a scene exceeds the default extent. The clearance field is bounded to four voxels and built together with occupancy. Each voxel stores the closest proxy's base albedo, emission, and signed distance. Before sampling, each proxy AABB is expanded by half a voxel on each axis, so voxel cells intersecting it become occupied; this preserves thin walls at the cost of up to one voxel of added thickness. `SDF occupied voxels` visualizes those occupied boundary cells.

The emission texture alpha channel stores a conservative clearance lower bound, capped at four voxels. A 26-neighbor chessboard distance transform measures distance in voxel layers to the nearest occupied cell; multiplying by the smallest voxel size and subtracting the voxel half-diagonal keeps the center value below the distance to occupied voxel volume. Since the grid is static, camera motion never propagates clearance or rebuilds the distance field. The shader subtracts the current sample's offset from its voxel center and uses this value only to skip empty space. Near geometry, grid traversal visits cells and confirms a hit from occupancy. Hit positions therefore have voxel-level precision, and normals remain gradients of the interpolated signed field. Trace termination is explicit: `HIT`, `MAX_STEPS`, `MAX_DISTANCE`, or `GRID_EXIT`. Only a hit evaluates proxy lighting, and only a completed maximum-distance trace contributes sky radiance. Step exhaustion and leaving the clipmap remain unresolved for radiance gathering: the coarse cache preserves an existing sample or leaves a new one invalid, and surface probes exclude unresolved rays. For direct-light visibility, `MAX_STEPS` remains blocked, while `GRID_EXIT` is visible because the scene-bounded SDF includes padding around all scene geometry. Coarse field rays travel at most 12 m, surface-probe rays 8 m, and direct-light rays stop at their light; each traversal is capped at 96 distance/voxel steps. The SDF represents per-instance AABBs, not source meshes: a concave, hollow, L-shaped, or thinly detailed mesh can become a solid box and create false occlusion. Conservative voxelization favors coverage over exact thickness, and per-geometry SDF resolution or a local high-resolution representation is not implemented.

The static scene-bounded radiance cache is one uniform world-space field with six directional lobes per cell. It is centered on active scene geometry bounds and never scrolls with the camera. The field starts at the SDF extent and expands per axis to contain the scene bounds plus 8 m horizontal and 4 m vertical padding, keeping the configured cell counts fixed as scenes grow. Its default resolution is 32 × 8 × 32 cells; Genos configures 40 × 10 × 40 cells. These settings double the cell count on each axis relative to the previous configuration, halving cell spacing at the same field extent. The renderer initializes the entire field in one compute update with a 24-ray snapshot per cell, then traces one ray per directional channel for every cell on every refinement update. When refinement is disabled, point- or spot-light changes queue cells within the union of the old and new light influence bounds ahead of other dirty cells, while geometry, sky, and directional-light changes queue the whole field. With refinement enabled, all cells update every frame, and scene or lighting changes are coalesced into snapshots at most once every 60 frames. Refinement therefore updates all cells each frame instead of waiting for a field sweep. On top of that baseline, cells whose centers fall inside the camera frustum receive a four-ray-per-channel snapshot within 8 m every 120 frames, within 24 m every 360 frames, or within 64 m every 900 frames. Cell-seeded frame offsets spread these snapshots across frames. Each snapshot contributes four additional rays per channel to the sample-weighted running average; its blend factor follows its four-sample batch weight relative to the accumulated history. Each change snapshot contributes four rays per channel to the existing running average without resetting its valid or attempted sample counts. Later one-ray refinements accumulate from that snapshot. With refinement disabled, automatic updates and camera snapshots stop; explicit invalidations still use the bounded queue. Static indexing keeps each cache cell associated with the same world position as the camera moves. During the first field update, all cells clear their storage and initialize from their 24-ray snapshots; camera movement does not re-anchor the field. Each cardinal channel (+X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, -Z) starts with four rays: one exactly on its axis and three deterministic, world-cell-seeded directions, one jittered sample in each of three equal-solid-angle hemisphere bands. All four rays have equal weight and contribute only to their assigned channel. The three non-axis samples use a sequence seeded by global world-cell position and channel, so each world-space cell keeps the same initial snapshot. Each refinement refresh traces one ray for every channel, for a maximum of six rays per cell. Resolved traces contribute to the running average; unresolved traces do not contribute to the radiance estimate, and the sample sequence advances so the next attempt uses a new direction. Explicit field initialization resets the per-cell sampling sequence. During normal refinement, resolved samples use a sample-weighted running average. Camera-priority snapshots add their four-ray-per-channel batch to the running average; subsequent refinement samples continue adding to the same history. A scene or lighting refresh uses a four-ray-per-channel snapshot for one frame and adds its resolved samples to the existing average; later one-ray refinements continue accumulating without resetting history. Unresolved rays advance the sample sequence but do not enter the radiance average. Progressive refinement is enabled by default in `RuntimeContext`; the `Refine samples` control changes the update schedule, while resolved radiance batches always accumulate. `RuntimeContext::freeze_gi_radiance` pauses scheduled and dirty-cell refreshes while retaining current field contents. `gi_radiance_temporal_accumulation_enabled` defaults to true and controls progressive refinement and camera-priority snapshots; it does not disable sample accumulation. The Lighting GI inspector exposes this toggle as `Refine samples`, the cache-freeze control as `Freeze cache updates`, and no receiver-to-probe visibility filter control remains. It also offers an explicit scene-pass GPU timestamp sample. Before each scheduled radiance update, the shader checks the cell center against the conservative SDF proxy and relocates ray origins toward free space until sampled clearance reaches 0.35 m, or relocation stops at a 1.0 m maximum offset. It follows the SDF gradient first and falls back to bounded six-axis samples when the gradient is degenerate. Cells that cannot find clearance remain invalid for that update. The shared GI inspector reports the original cell center, ray origin, and offset; the selected-ray gizmo shows the original center with a cyan cross, the relocated origin with a green cross, and the displacement with an amber segment. A failed relocation uses a red origin cross. Final AABB-SDF raster shading uses only this field; screen-space surface-probe gathering is disabled by default and is not part of the final shading path, while its implementation and cache remain available for comparison. Shading computes trilinear weights for the eight surrounding cells, rejects negligible weights and invalid relocation records, and blends the remaining valid directional radiance. The receiver normal weights the six directional lobes and applies a soft same-side weight to each relocated probe using the offset shading point. The 0.2 m smooth transition reduces probes behind the receiver plane but does not guarantee geometric line of sight. At default resolution, directional values use about 768 KiB and trace diagnostics use about 39 MiB. Genos uses about 1.5 MiB for directional values and 76 MiB for trace diagnostics. This is a uniform cache, not Radiance Cascades: it has no interval hierarchy or higher-order bounce transport. It can still inherit proxy-SDF errors and angular banding, receiver-to-probe visibility traversal cost has been removed from the fragment path. `RendererPerformanceStats` reports SDF CPU build time, upload preparation/enqueue time, rebuilt voxel count, and the radiance-field compute GPU time; the legacy `gi_history_valid_percent` statistic now reports field availability after a completed update. GPU pass timings refresh when a scripted report is read or when the inspector requests a one-shot GPU timing sample.

The current diagnostic and final field shading sample at `P_surface + N * 0.1 m`. Final field shading uses trilinear spatial weights for the surrounding cells, applies a smooth same-side weight using the receiver normal and each relocated probe position, and cosine-weights each valid cell's six directional radiance lobes by the receiver normal. The side weight is smoothstep over a 0.2 m range centered on the offset shading point. It reduces contributions from probes behind the receiver plane, but does not verify line of sight through arbitrary geometry. A relocation record marks whether each probe has a valid ray origin, and invalid probes are excluded from interpolation independently of this same-side weighting.

The six cache axes are +X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, and -Z. The initial four-ray-per-channel hemisphere snapshot is described above. Each scheduled refinement update traces one new ray for every channel, up to six rays per cell, regardless of how similar recent samples are. The previous stability check and per-channel attempt limit are disabled, so resolved samples continue to accumulate for as long as refinement remains enabled. Resolved samples update the stored radiance through a sample-weighted running average; the shader does not clear sample history when the low-discrepancy sequence wraps or when it receives a periodic snapshot. Unresolved traces do not enter the radiance estimate and their directions are retried later in the sequence. Each fixed world-space cell owns a saturating deterministic round counter, and each channel advances its low-discrepancy sample sequence from its attempt count. Camera-priority, scene, and lighting snapshots add their resolved rays to the existing channel sample count and running average; none reset the prior sample history. The currently selected inspector cell is reserved a slot each frame while urgent queues are active. When refinement is disabled, queued initial and dirty work remains limited to 16 cells per field and frame. New and light-priority cells receive one follow-up batch when refinement is disabled. With refinement enabled, all cells update each frame, while in-frustum cells receive distance-tiered snapshots (8 m / 120 frames, 24 m / 360 frames, 64 m / 900 frames) added to their prior radiance using the sample-weighted running average. Scene and lighting refreshes are coalesced to one four-ray-per-channel snapshot per 60 frames, then resume additive one-ray refinement. When refinement is disabled, automatic updates stop; scene invalidations still queue fresh samples.

The retained screen-space surface-probe path owns a persistent cache for comparison, but its updates default to disabled and final AABB-SDF raster shading does not gather from it. The depth prepass writes geometric normals to an `Rgba16Float` target and per-instance IDs to an `R32Uint` target. The current renderer uses its unjittered camera projection for these samples. A compute grid evaluates one candidate per 16 × 16 tile and keeps only tiles on a deterministic 32 × 32 screen lattice; edge refinement is disabled. A deterministic budget selects up to 3,000 visible base probes per frame, distributing selection evenly across the lattice at resolutions where it exceeds that limit. Each selected tile maps directly to a unique cache slot by its lattice index, including under budget subsampling, so GPU workgroup order cannot change slot ownership or the stable ray-sampling seed. A slot retains its radiance only while its surface ID, geometric normal, and world position remain compatible; otherwise it is reinitialized. The gizmo pass shows probes selected this frame, with their normal segments colored by radiance. Anchors use the exact sampled surface position and geometric normal; rays start 8 cm along the geometric normal. A probe keeps the world-space anchor associated with its stored radiance until it traces again. Movement beyond one quarter SDF voxel forces an immediate trace and replaces the old value; smaller movement reduces the probe's confidence without dragging its anchor. The stable slot index seeds a fixed sampling rotation. New probes trace immediately and stable base probes update every 16 frames. Reused radiance blends at 0.65 for base probes, 0.8 for priority probes, and 0.9 for high-priority probes; a moved anchor replaces its old sample. Slots not selected for up to 240 frames remain available before eviction. Each updated probe traces four diffuse SDF rays. A hit combines proxy direct/emissive lighting with the coarse radiance field; a miss uses sky radiance. The existing full-resolution gather still searches a fixed 5 × 5 neighborhood of the screen lattice and blends up to the 12 nearest compatible probes, but the current final shading path no longer calls it. For screen-probe visibility, both ray endpoints are offset along the receiver normal so the visibility segment does not enter the receiving surface; the coarse-field visibility check retains its cell-center endpoint. The SDF still represents instance AABBs, and both paths need profiling on larger scenes.

Visible surface candidates use deterministic tile-index selection and deterministic cache-slot ownership on the fixed 32 × 32 base lattice, so a fixed depth/normal/ID buffer selects the same tiles and ray seeds every frame even when GPU workgroup scheduling changes. Raster shading searches a fixed 5 × 5 screen-lattice neighborhood, avoiding a concurrent spatial-hash chain. Edge refinement is currently disabled. The lighting inspector selects a cell in the single radiance field and one of its 24 rays; the selected ray is the default display, and `All-ray overview` adds the other 23 rays at reduced opacity. Four ray pages are grouped under each cardinal channel. Pick selects the nearest radiance cell to a clicked mesh surface; W/S, A/D, and R/F move one field cell along Z, X, and Y. The inspector reads field resolution and world bounds from the renderer and displays cell count, world-space field size, per-cell spacing, and selected cell center. Geometry mode colors paths by hit/miss, shows the endpoint, hit normal, termination reason, and travelled distance. Radiance mode uses a neutral path, tone-mapped trace-time radiance at hits, a blue normal, and an orange marker for non-hit traces. It displays raw RGB plus direct, emission, and sky contributions before cache accumulation, the currently stored lobe RGB, signed raw-minus-stored RGB difference, and age in field-update frames. It also shows the selected ray direction, accumulated sample count for the corresponding fixed lobe, and blend factor. The renderer reads the stored value from the single field buffer and retains the last update frame beside the raw trace record; this diagnostic is asynchronous. A selected ray's raw trace is refreshed when its cell is refreshed. Every resolved batch, including periodic snapshots, blends into the accumulated lobe using its valid sample count; the `Refine samples` control changes the update schedule but never switches the cache to replacement mode. The inspector reports hit position and normal from the same trace record. Only a completed `MAX_DISTANCE` trace receives sky radiance; `MAX_STEPS` and `GRID_EXIT` remain unresolved and report zero sky. A cell outline highlights the selected field cell. Other gizmo categories, including screen probes and SDF occupancy boxes, are hidden in Radiance mode unless `Show other gizmos` is enabled. `SDF occupied voxels` separately draws translucent boxes for occupied voxels that border empty cells, showing the field boundary without drawing every interior voxel. The 3,000-probe limit evenly subsamples the surface-probe lattice only when it exceeds the visible probe budget.

## Public contract

The default levels cover 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, 64 × 48 × 64 at 8 × 5 × 8 probes, and 128 × 96 × 128 at 6 × 4 × 6 probes. The near level has tighter vertical spacing and a larger horizontal footprint; probe density falls at each larger scale. The three levels use 2,830 probes total; combined with the 1,008-probe room-test volume they fit within the 4,096-probe capacity and four descriptors.

Applications call `context.irradiance_volumes.add(IrradianceVolumeDesc { ... })` during setup and retain the returned `IrradianceVolumeId` when they need to mutate the description. They can set `context.irradiance_mode` to `Volumes`, `Clipmap`, `Combined`, or experimental `AabbSdf`, configure irradiance clipmap levels through `context.irradiance_clipmap`, and set the single field's cell counts through `context.gi_radiance_field_resolution` before renderer initialization. Each axis is clamped to 1–40 cells horizontally and 1–10 vertically. AABB-SDF mode bypasses probe volumes; field rays evaluate proxy material, direct lights, emission, and sky. `RuntimeContext::gi_trace_diagnostics` is the latest asynchronously read back set of six raw trace results and matching stored lobe samples for the selected cell, captured before cache interpolation; each record includes raw and stored RGB, signed difference, last-update age, sampled ray direction, fixed-lobe resolved/attempted sample counts, and blend factor. `RuntimeContext::gizmos_enabled` is the master switch for overlays and defaults to off; the individual normal, light, global GI, screen probe, trace, and SDF occupancy toggles also default to off. `normal_gizmos_enabled`, `light_gizmos_enabled`, and `gi_global_gizmos_enabled` select mesh normals, light markers, volume/clipmap markers, and the world radiance field; `gi_gizmos_enabled` selects visible screen-driven surface probes. `gi_trace_gizmos_enabled` displays six traces from the cell selected by `gi_selected_radiance_cell`; in the Lighting example, opening the Selected GI cell rays popout enables these traces and master gizmo visibility until it closes. The popout contains cell and ray selection, raw/stored radiance values, signed difference, update age, sampling direction and accumulation diagnostics, cache freeze, refinement, and a one-shot scene GPU timing sample button. `sdf_occupancy_gizmos_enabled` draws SDF boundary voxels. `freeze_gi_radiance` remains a diagnostic switch for app or capture control: it pauses coarse world-field refresh while retaining current field contents. The Lighting GI inspector exposes a cache-freeze control for validation. Pending scripted captures suppress all overlays. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_update_budget` controls the maximum number of probes refreshed per frame in probe modes. `RuntimeContext::sky_lighting` controls sky radiance for probe updates and SDF misses. `RuntimeContext::performance_stats` exposes probe update time and counts plus SDF build/upload time, updated voxel count, radiance-field compute time, scene-pass GPU time, and field availability (retained under the legacy `gi_history_valid_percent` metric name). `RuntimeContext::request_gpu_timing_sample` is a one-shot engine request consumed after rendering; it blocks for one timestamp readback. GPU time is also refreshed by scripted timestamp reports. `context.gizmos` exposes line, wire-box, ring, dot, and sampled mesh-normal helpers for additional diagnostics.

The renderer returns six asynchronous diagnostics for the selected field cell. Each record includes trace-time radiance components, termination reason, travelled distance, hit position, and hit normal when a hit occurs. `RuntimeContext::gi_trace_selected_ray` selects a ray from 0 through 5; `gi_trace_debug_mode` selects `Geometry` or `Radiance`; `gi_trace_overview_enabled` defaults to false and displays the other rays at reduced opacity when enabled. The lighting inspector owns these controls as a projection of `RuntimeContext` state. Radiance mode uses a neutral ray line, radiance-colored cross dot at hits, blue normal, and orange miss marker; Geometry mode retains hit/miss path colors and termination/distance markers. `RenderDebugMode::GiSupport` colors receiving surfaces from single-field support: red shows the fraction of valid cells, green the remaining spatial weight, and blue directional support; magenta means no usable samples. Gray means the world radiance field is unavailable. Opening Radiance mode suppresses other gizmo categories by default; the inspector's Show other gizmos toggle is the explicit override.

The shared GI Surface Inspector requests the clicked viewport pixel and evaluates it in a renderer compute pass after the depth prepass. The compute pass reconstructs world position from depth and the camera inverse view-projection matrix, reads the world normal, and calls the same interpolation-candidate and directional-lobe helpers as final raster GI. Both paths use each candidate's relocated probe position to apply a soft same-side weight: the dot product of the receiver normal and the vector from the offset shading point to the relocated probe is mapped through a 0.2 m smooth transition. This reduces the weight of probes behind the receiving surface, then normalizes the remaining weights. It is a receiver-plane heuristic, not a segment visibility test, so it does not guarantee separation around corners or for ambiguous thin geometry.


## Lifecycle

The engine creates the volume set, clipmap configuration, and default radiance-field resolution before application setup. Applications add or mutate authored volumes, select a GI mode, and may override the single radiance-field resolution during setup. Renderer initialization snapshots that resolution and scene geometry bounds anchor the static SDF and radiance grids. When instance geometry changes the scene bounds, the renderer re-centers and resizes the grids as needed, clears re-anchored radiance data, and schedules fresh samples. Each redraw passes the active camera, scene instances, and lights to the renderer. In AABB-SDF mode the renderer rebuilds and uploads the full SDF only when scene geometry changes, schedules a fixed cell budget for coarse radiance, and shades from the scene field using trilinear interpolation. Camera movement changes neither grid bounds nor cell ownership. Surface-probe refresh and maintenance dispatches are skipped by default; the existing cache and shader path remain available for comparison. Optional diagnostics draw the six traces from the selected field cell and the boundary voxels whose signed distance marks them occupied. Resize recreates screen-sized targets and updates the screen sampling grid without discarding probe-cache allocations. Geometry, light, sky, or mode changes enqueue field refresh work. The room-test point light can be moved with I/J/K/L/U/O, while WASD moves the camera. The field and its diagnostic resources are released with the renderer.

## Verification and open questions

Each radiance cell has a compact relocation record containing the effective ray origin and a status value. The compute path writes this record when it relocates a probe; field interpolation excludes records with a non-positive status, including after geometry changes clear the records and before refresh completes. The relocation state is for probe validity and ray origin ownership; the soft same-side factor is evaluated per receiving surface and is not stored in the relocation record.

The six directional radiance lobes and uniform scene-sized field provide approximate indirect lighting across the active scene, but cannot remove errors from per-instance AABB proxies or low spatial resolution. Conservative voxelization covers geometry that falls between SDF samples by expanding proxy bounds by half a voxel per axis; this increases apparent thickness and can add shadowing around thin walls. Both receiver-to-probe filtering approaches previously prototyped here—the per-pixel SDF traversal and six-direction DDGI-style depth moments—have been removed. The current final field uses trilinear interpolation among valid probes, soft same-side weighting from the receiver plane, and receiver-normal weighting across the six radiance lobes. This reduces obvious through-wall contributors but remains a heuristic, so leakage around corners and thin geometry can persist. The SDF remains active for radiance-ray intersection, direct visibility, and probe relocation. The retained fixed-lattice probe gather may still be useful for later comparison, but it is not part of the current final shading path. This prototype does not implement Radiance Cascade interval hierarchy, multi-bounce transport, or motion reprojection. The separate authored and clipmap probe modes still use CPU evaluation and retain their 4,096-probe and four-descriptor limits.

Verification: `cargo check -p renderer -p labyrinth -p labyrinth_debug_ui -p genos -p lighting -p primitives`, `cargo fmt --all -- --check`, and `git diff --check` passed. `cargo run -p lighting -- --room-test` created the Vulkan renderer and presented its first frame without wgpu shader or bind-group validation errors. The inspector's voxel bounds and translucent wire-box overlay still need visual inspection after picking a surface; the diagnostic does not yet decide whether contiguous occupied voxels belong to the receiver or a separate blocker.

The large-scene single-field resize test and renderer compilation were previously checked. The Genos small/large modes and the full single-field shader pipeline should be rechecked after this change. Red-box bleed, wall/doorway visibility, camera movement, scene-wide field coverage, and fragment GPU cost have not yet been visually validated or profiled.
