# Irradiance volumes

**Status:** Implemented authored volumes and a camera-following three-level variable-density clipmap, selectable volume/clipmap/combined modes, and experimental AABB-SDF GI with one uniform scene-sized radiance field using equal-area angular segments; screen-space probes are retained but disabled by default
**Owner:** `crates/engine` configuration and `crates/renderer` GPU drawing
**Source:** [gi.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gi.rs), [gizmo.rs](../../crates/engine/src/gizmo.rs), [renderer lib.rs](../../crates/renderer/src/lib.rs), [shader.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/shader.wgsl), [gi_gizmo.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/gi_gizmo.wgsl), [depth_prepass.wgsl](../../crates/renderer/src/depth_prepass.wgsl)

## Purpose and boundary

`IrradianceVolumes` owns app-created world-space volume descriptions. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_mode` selects authored volumes, a camera-following irradiance clipmap, both, or experimental AABB-SDF GI. The nested clipmap extents and resolutions come from `RuntimeContext::irradiance_clipmap`; each level snaps its center to that level's probe spacing. The renderer owns the authored-probe cache, bounded CPU updates, cached surface samples, a coarse visibility grid, and normal-facing probe sampling. AABB-SDF mode bypasses probe volumes and their update path. It uses a static AABB distance/material field and one static scene-bounded radiance field for final raster GI. The field resolution defaults to 32 × 8 × 32 cells and applications can override it through `RuntimeContext::gi_radiance_field_resolution` during setup. Its extent is at least the SDF extent and expands as needed to cover active scene geometry bounds plus padding. The configured cell counts stay fixed after initialization, so spacing can increase when the field expands. Geometry-driven bounds changes re-center the grids and clear radiance data before refreshing; camera movement does not scroll them. The renderer publishes the current field bounds and resolution through `RuntimeContext::gi_radiance_field_grid` for world-position-to-cell inspection. The retained fixed-lattice surface-probe cache is disabled by default and is not sampled by final raster shading. Raster shading samples the world field instead of tracing a GI ray for every pixel.

## State and invariants

Each volume has minimum and maximum world-space bounds and a three-dimensional probe resolution. Three nested clipmap levels are centered on the active camera. Their extents grow as probe densities decrease; each level snaps independently, preserves cached values at overlapping world-space probe positions, and schedules newly exposed probes through the normal bounded refresh budget. The near level covers 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, keeping common floor and wall receivers inside the fine level before the coarse transition begins. Its even vertical resolution places the room-test floor between probe rows at the tested camera height, avoiding a coplanar row at the floor. The shader favors earlier, finer volumes in their interior and cross-fades to later, coarser volumes over two local probe spacings near a volume boundary. If a level has no valid front-facing trilinear support, its blend weight transfers to later volumes. Authored volumes therefore retain priority in combined mode away from their boundaries, while clipmap levels provide smooth overlap and coarser fallback support. Irradiance data uses a read-only storage buffer with a 4,096-probe capacity and four volume descriptors; combined mode reserves one descriptor per active clipmap level. Disabled authored volumes are skipped. Each refreshed probe stores sky visibility in its alpha channel and a weighted dominant incoming direction in a second compact buffer. The irradiance cache stores evaluated probe positions and a validity bit; connected-region labels remain in the CPU visibility grid. The renderer caches samples from static meshes until their transforms or materials change, rebuilds dynamic samples for moving meshes, voxelizes world-space mesh bounds, computes a distance field and connected free-space region labels, and retains those labels for CPU-side probe placement and bounce filtering. Probe locations inside occupied cells are invalidated; probes near geometry can move only to a clear cell in their original region. Directly lit and emissive surface samples use distance-weighted contributions, per-instance sample normalization, and probe-to-surface visibility tests. The shader offsets horizontal receivers by three quarters of local probe spacing along their normal and near-vertical receivers by one quarter. For near-vertical receivers it also shifts the interpolation point upward by one half of vertical probe spacing, keeping wall samples near floor joins off the floor-aligned probe row. The validity bit excludes probes invalidated by geometry from both the color sample and the interpolation normalization. The shader excludes probes whose signed distance from the receiver plane is zero or negative, blends front-side trilinear support, and attenuates probes by their angular alignment with the normal, so nearly perpendicular probes contribute less. Color and directional estimates are normalized by the same angularly weighted support, avoiding a darkening when only a small subset of front-facing probes contribute. Probes behind the surface are excluded, while probes in front remain available across connected-region boundaries. Connected-region labels still constrain probe relocation and CPU-side bounce accumulation. The renderer sphere-traces five axis-aligned rays per refreshed probe. Emissive contributions also add their source direction to the compact directional response. Distance-field rebuilds are throttled while geometry moves, and probe lighting continues on its bounded per-frame schedule. Refreshed values are blended into the cache to hide individual-probe update boundaries. Stale probes near the active camera are prioritized.

Clipmap samples are trilinearly interpolated within each level, then cross-faded across the shared bounds. The transition width accounts for the finer and coarser spacing and is capped by their overlap, avoiding a short band tuned only to the fine grid. Probe-to-surface and local-light-to-surface visibility rays end just outside the sampled surface and use a tight endpoint tolerance derived from the smallest visibility-grid cell dimension, so an obstruction near the target is not automatically treated as visible.

AABB-SDF GI is an experimental probe-free, single-bounce path. Its 128 × 32 × 128 grid defaults to 64 × 16 × 64 world units and expands per axis to cover larger scene bounds plus 8 m horizontal and 4 m vertical padding. The room-test remains at the default extent, with 16 m horizontal padding around its 32 m floor and outer walls. The SDF voxel count stays fixed, so larger scenes have coarser voxels. Camera movement does not move the SDF bounds or rebuild occupancy. When scene geometry bounds change, the field re-centers to the new bounds and rebuilds in full; geometry is no longer clipped just because a scene exceeds the default extent. The clearance field is bounded to four voxels and built together with occupancy. Each voxel stores the closest proxy's base albedo, emission, and signed distance. Before sampling, each proxy AABB is expanded by half a voxel on each axis, so voxel cells intersecting it become occupied; this preserves thin walls at the cost of up to one voxel of added thickness. `SDF occupied voxels` visualizes those occupied boundary cells.

The emission texture alpha channel stores a conservative clearance lower bound, capped at four voxels. A 26-neighbor chessboard distance transform measures distance in voxel layers to the nearest occupied cell; multiplying by the smallest voxel size and subtracting the voxel half-diagonal keeps the center value below the distance to occupied voxel volume. Since the grid is static, camera motion never propagates clearance or rebuilds the distance field. The shader subtracts the current sample's offset from its voxel center and uses this value only to skip empty space. Near geometry, grid traversal visits cells and confirms a hit from occupancy. Hit positions therefore have voxel-level precision, and normals remain gradients of the interpolated signed field. Trace termination is explicit: `HIT`, `MAX_STEPS`, `MAX_DISTANCE`, or `GRID_EXIT`. Only a hit evaluates proxy lighting, and only a completed maximum-distance trace contributes sky radiance. Step exhaustion and leaving the clipmap remain unresolved for radiance gathering: the coarse cache preserves an existing sample or leaves a new one invalid, and surface probes exclude unresolved rays. For direct-light visibility, `MAX_STEPS` remains blocked, while `GRID_EXIT` is visible because the scene-bounded SDF includes padding around all scene geometry. Coarse field rays travel at most 12 m, surface-probe rays 8 m, and direct-light rays stop at their light; each traversal is capped at 96 distance/voxel steps. The SDF represents per-instance AABBs, not source meshes: a concave, hollow, L-shaped, or thinly detailed mesh can become a solid box and create false occlusion. Conservative voxelization favors coverage over exact thickness, and per-geometry SDF resolution or a local high-resolution representation is not implemented.

The static scene-bounded radiance cache is one uniform world-space field, centered on active scene geometry bounds and fixed in world space as the camera moves. It starts at the SDF extent and expands to cover scene bounds plus 8 m horizontal and 4 m vertical padding; cell counts remain fixed. Its default resolution is 32 × 8 × 32 cells; Genos configures 40 × 10 × 40. Each cell stores up to 64 equal-solid-angle regions, partitioned into 2 × 4, 4 × 8, or 8 × 8 bands and sectors. The default is 32 regions. The inspector can select 8, 32, or 64 regions; changing resolution clears and refreshes the field. Each region owns its radiance mean, resolved sample count, attempt count, and last-update frame. Every cell update traces two jittered rays from opposite ends of the round-robin region order, giving all regions equal sampling opportunity over time. The 8-, 32-, and 64-region settings therefore need at least 4, 16, and 32 cell updates respectively to visit every region once. Resolved samples update only their region with a sample-weighted mean; unresolved rays advance that region’s attempt sequence but do not affect its mean. Shading cosine-weights region-center radiance over the equal-area partition and reports the fraction of angular support with valid samples. Lighting and geometry invalidation retain bounded cell scheduling; the selected cell exposes its latest two trace records for inspection. The renderer stores a fixed 64-slot layout per cell, about 16 MiB at the default field resolution and 32 MiB for Genos, plus two trace diagnostics per cell. AABB-SDF raster shading still blends the valid field cells trilinearly and applies the receiver-plane same-side weight; this is not Radiance Cascades and does not add multi-bounce transport.

The current diagnostic and final field shading sample at `P_surface + N * 0.1 m`. Final field shading uses trilinear spatial weights for the surrounding cells, applies a smooth same-side weight using the receiver normal and each relocated probe position, and cosine-weights each valid cell's equal-area angular-region radiance by the receiver normal. The side weight is smoothstep over a 0.2 m range centered on the offset shading point. It reduces contributions from probes behind the receiver plane, but does not verify line of sight through arbitrary geometry. A relocation record marks whether each probe has a valid ray origin, and invalid probes are excluded from interpolation independently of this same-side weighting.

Each angular segment is an equal-area estimator rather than a broad directional lobe. A running cursor advances one region per cell update, with one ray sampling from each end of the region order. There are no larger initialization, refresh, or camera-priority batches. With refinement enabled, every cell traces two rays per update; with refinement disabled, explicit invalidations continue through the bounded work queue, also tracing two rays per scheduled cell update. The selected-cell debug view reads back both latest traces and all 64 region records. The selected-cell sphere is opaque grayscale: a resolved sample turns its region white, then it fades exponentially toward black with a 180-frame time constant. Unsampled and unresolved regions stay black, and failed traces do not refresh a region’s age.

The retained screen-space surface-probe path owns a persistent cache for comparison, but its updates default to disabled and final AABB-SDF raster shading does not gather from it. The depth prepass writes geometric normals to an `Rgba16Float` target and per-instance IDs to an `R32Uint` target. The current renderer uses its unjittered camera projection for these samples. A compute grid evaluates one candidate per 16 × 16 tile and keeps only tiles on a deterministic 32 × 32 screen lattice; edge refinement is disabled. A deterministic budget selects up to 3,000 visible base probes per frame, distributing selection evenly across the lattice at resolutions where it exceeds that limit. Each selected tile maps directly to a unique cache slot by its lattice index, including under budget subsampling, so GPU workgroup order cannot change slot ownership or the stable ray-sampling seed. A slot retains its radiance only while its surface ID, geometric normal, and world position remain compatible; otherwise it is reinitialized. The gizmo pass shows probes selected this frame, with their normal segments colored by radiance. Anchors use the exact sampled surface position and geometric normal; rays start 8 cm along the geometric normal. A probe keeps the world-space anchor associated with its stored radiance until it traces again. Movement beyond one quarter SDF voxel forces an immediate trace and replaces the old value; smaller movement reduces the probe's confidence without dragging its anchor. The stable slot index seeds a fixed sampling rotation. New probes trace immediately and stable base probes update every 16 frames. Reused radiance blends at 0.65 for base probes, 0.8 for priority probes, and 0.9 for high-priority probes; a moved anchor replaces its old sample. Slots not selected for up to 240 frames remain available before eviction. Each updated probe traces four diffuse SDF rays. A hit combines proxy direct/emissive lighting with the coarse radiance field; a miss uses sky radiance. The existing full-resolution gather still searches a fixed 5 × 5 neighborhood of the screen lattice and blends up to the 12 nearest compatible probes, but the current final shading path no longer calls it. For screen-probe visibility, both ray endpoints are offset along the receiver normal so the visibility segment does not enter the receiving surface; the coarse-field visibility check retains its cell-center endpoint. The SDF still represents instance AABBs, and both paths need profiling on larger scenes.

Visible surface candidates use deterministic tile-index selection and deterministic cache-slot ownership on the fixed 32 × 32 base lattice, so a fixed depth/normal/ID buffer selects the same tiles and ray seeds every frame even when GPU workgroup scheduling changes. Raster shading searches a fixed 5 × 5 screen-lattice neighborhood, avoiding a concurrent spatial-hash chain. Edge refinement is currently disabled. The lighting inspector selects a cell in the single radiance field and shows its latest two rays, labeled R0 and R1; both paths appear in the gizmo while the selection controls the detailed readout. Pick selects the nearest radiance cell to a clicked mesh surface; W/S, A/D, and R/F move one field cell along Z, X, and Y. The inspector reads field resolution and world bounds from the renderer and displays cell count, world-space field size, per-cell spacing, and selected cell center. Geometry mode colors paths by hit/miss, shows the endpoint, hit normal, termination reason, and travelled distance. Radiance mode uses a neutral path, tone-mapped trace-time radiance at hits, a blue normal, and an orange marker for non-hit traces. It displays raw RGB plus direct, emission, and sky contributions before cache accumulation, the currently stored region RGB, signed raw-minus-stored RGB difference, and age in field-update frames. It also shows the ray direction, accumulated sample count for the corresponding angular region, and blend factor. The renderer reads the stored value from the single field buffer and retains the last update frame beside the raw trace record; this diagnostic is asynchronous and refreshes with each cell update. Each resolved ray blends into its region using its valid sample count; the `Refine samples` control changes the update schedule but never switches the cache to replacement mode. The inspector reports hit position and normal from the same trace record. Only a completed `MAX_DISTANCE` trace receives sky radiance; `MAX_STEPS` and `GRID_EXIT` remain unresolved and report zero sky. A cell outline highlights the selected field cell. Other gizmo categories, including screen probes and SDF occupancy boxes, are hidden in Radiance mode unless `Show other gizmos` is enabled. `SDF occupied voxels` separately draws translucent boxes for occupied voxels that border empty cells, showing the field boundary without drawing every interior voxel. The 3,000-probe limit evenly subsamples the surface-probe lattice only when it exceeds the visible probe budget.

## Public contract

The default levels cover 40 × 20 × 40 at 13 × 14 × 13 probes, 64 × 48 × 64 at 8 × 5 × 8 probes, and 128 × 96 × 128 at 6 × 4 × 6 probes. The near level has tighter vertical spacing and a larger horizontal footprint; probe density falls at each larger scale. The three levels use 2,830 probes total; combined with the 1,008-probe room-test volume they fit within the 4,096-probe capacity and four descriptors.

Applications call `context.irradiance_volumes.add(IrradianceVolumeDesc { ... })` during setup and retain the returned `IrradianceVolumeId` when they need to mutate the description. They can set `context.irradiance_mode` to `Volumes`, `Clipmap`, `Combined`, or experimental `AabbSdf`, configure irradiance clipmap levels through `context.irradiance_clipmap`, and set the single field's cell counts through `context.gi_radiance_field_resolution` before renderer initialization. Each axis is clamped to 1–40 cells horizontally and 1–10 vertically. AABB-SDF mode bypasses probe volumes; field rays evaluate proxy material, direct lights, emission, and sky. `RuntimeContext::gi_trace_diagnostics` contains the latest asynchronously read back pair of raw trace results and matching stored angular-region samples for the selected cell, captured before cache interpolation; the record includes raw and stored RGB, signed difference, last-update age, sampled ray direction, region resolved/attempted sample counts, and blend factor. `RuntimeContext::gizmos_enabled` is the master switch for overlays and defaults to off; the individual normal, light, global GI, screen probe, trace, and SDF occupancy toggles also default to off. `normal_gizmos_enabled`, `light_gizmos_enabled`, and `gi_global_gizmos_enabled` select mesh normals, light markers, volume/clipmap markers, and the world radiance field; `gi_gizmos_enabled` selects visible screen-driven surface probes. `gi_trace_gizmos_enabled` displays both latest rays from the cell selected by `gi_selected_radiance_cell`; in the Lighting example, opening the Selected GI cell rays popout enables these traces and master gizmo visibility until it closes. The popout contains cell selection, raw/stored radiance values, signed difference, update age, sampling direction and accumulation diagnostics, cache freeze, refinement, and a one-shot scene GPU timing sample button. `sdf_occupancy_gizmos_enabled` draws SDF boundary voxels. `freeze_gi_radiance` remains a diagnostic switch for app or capture control: it pauses coarse world-field refresh while retaining current field contents. The Lighting GI inspector exposes a cache-freeze control for validation. Pending scripted captures suppress all overlays. `RuntimeContext::irradiance_update_budget` controls the maximum number of probes refreshed per frame in probe modes. `RuntimeContext::sky_lighting` controls sky radiance for probe updates and SDF misses. `RuntimeContext::performance_stats` exposes probe update time and counts plus SDF build/upload time, updated voxel count, radiance-field compute time, scene-pass GPU time, and field availability (retained under the legacy `gi_history_valid_percent` metric name). `RuntimeContext::request_gpu_timing_sample` is a one-shot engine request consumed after rendering; it blocks for one timestamp readback. GPU time is also refreshed by scripted timestamp reports. `context.gizmos` exposes line, wire-box, ring, dot, and sampled mesh-normal helpers for additional diagnostics.

The renderer returns two asynchronous trace diagnostics for the selected field cell, updated with each cell sample. Each record includes trace-time radiance components, termination reason, travelled distance, hit position, and hit normal when a hit occurs. `RuntimeContext::gi_trace_debug_mode` selects `Geometry` or `Radiance`. The lighting inspector also displays the selected cell’s 8, 32, or 64 angular region estimates as paged RGB/sample rows and an opaque freshness sphere: resolved regions turn white and fade toward black over time, while unsampled or unresolved regions are black. The lighting inspector owns these controls as a projection of `RuntimeContext` state. Radiance mode uses a neutral ray line, radiance-colored cross dot at hits, blue normal, and orange miss marker; Geometry mode retains hit/miss path colors and termination/distance markers. `RenderDebugMode::GiSupport` colors receiving surfaces from single-field support: red shows the fraction of valid cells, green the remaining spatial weight, and blue angular support; magenta means no usable samples. Gray means the world radiance field is unavailable. Opening Radiance mode suppresses other gizmo categories by default; the inspector's Show other gizmos toggle is the explicit override.

The shared GI Surface Inspector requests the clicked viewport pixel and evaluates it in a renderer compute pass after the depth prepass. The compute pass reconstructs world position from depth and the camera inverse view-projection matrix, reads the world normal, and calls the same interpolation-candidate and angular-region helpers as final raster GI. Both paths use each candidate's relocated probe position to apply a soft same-side weight: the dot product of the receiver normal and the vector from the offset shading point to the relocated probe is mapped through a 0.2 m smooth transition. This reduces the weight of probes behind the receiving surface, then normalizes the remaining weights. It is a receiver-plane heuristic, not a segment visibility test, so it does not guarantee separation around corners or for ambiguous thin geometry.


## Lifecycle

The engine creates the volume set, clipmap configuration, and default radiance-field resolution before application setup. Applications add or mutate authored volumes, select a GI mode, and may override the single radiance-field resolution during setup. Renderer initialization snapshots that resolution and scene geometry bounds anchor the static SDF and radiance grids. When instance geometry changes the scene bounds, the renderer re-centers and resizes the grids as needed, clears re-anchored radiance data, and schedules fresh samples. Each redraw passes the active camera, scene instances, and lights to the renderer. In AABB-SDF mode the renderer rebuilds and uploads the full SDF only when scene geometry changes, schedules a fixed cell budget for coarse radiance, and shades from the scene field using trilinear interpolation. Each scheduled cell traces two counter-sweeping rays to update two angular regions; camera position does not trigger a larger sampling batch. Camera movement changes neither grid bounds nor cell ownership. Surface-probe refresh and maintenance dispatches are skipped by default; the existing cache and shader path remain available for comparison. Optional diagnostics draw both latest rays from the selected field cell and the boundary voxels whose signed distance marks them occupied. Resize recreates screen-sized targets and updates the screen sampling grid without discarding probe-cache allocations. Geometry, light, sky, or mode changes enqueue field refresh work. The room-test point light can be moved with I/J/K/L/U/O, while WASD moves the camera. The field and its diagnostic resources are released with the renderer.

## Verification and open questions

Each radiance cell has a compact relocation record containing the effective ray origin and a status value. The compute path writes this record when it relocates a probe; field interpolation excludes records with a non-positive status, including after geometry changes clear the records and before refresh completes. The relocation state is for probe validity and ray origin ownership; the soft same-side factor is evaluated per receiving surface and is not stored in the relocation record.

The equal-area angular field improves capture of small bright directions by keeping samples isolated in smaller regions. The 32-region default balances angular detail with gradual convergence; 8 regions trades detail for faster coverage, while 64 regions can resolve finer patches but needs more refinement frames to cover and stabilize the sphere. The field still inherits per-instance AABB proxy errors, conservative voxel thickness, and low spatial resolution. Final shading uses trilinear interpolation among valid cells, receiver-plane same-side weighting, and cosine-weighted integration over the angular regions. It does not implement Radiance Cascade interval hierarchy, multi-bounce transport, or motion reprojection.

Verification: `cargo check -p renderer -p labyrinth -p labyrinth_debug_ui -p genos -p lighting -p primitives`, `cargo fmt --all -- --check`, and `git diff --check` passed. `cargo run -p lighting -- --room-test` created the Vulkan renderer and presented its first frame without wgpu shader or bind-group validation errors. The inspector's voxel bounds and translucent wire-box overlay still need visual inspection after picking a surface; the diagnostic does not yet decide whether contiguous occupied voxels belong to the receiver or a separate blocker.

The large-scene single-field resize test and renderer compilation were previously checked. The Genos small/large modes and the full single-field shader pipeline should be rechecked after this change. Red-box bleed, wall/doorway visibility, camera movement, scene-wide field coverage, and fragment GPU cost have not yet been visually validated or profiled.
