# Rendering

**Status:** Implemented  
**Owner:** `crates/renderer`  
**Source:** [Renderer](../../crates/renderer/src/lib.rs), [mesh contract](../../crates/mesh/src/lib.rs), [diffuse shader](../../crates/renderer/src/shader.wgsl)

## Purpose and boundary

The experimental AABB-SDF GI path renders depth, geometric normals, and per-instance surface IDs before updating its coarse camera-centered radiance fields and persistent surface-probe cache. The cache samples a fixed 32 × 32 screen lattice; edge refinement is disabled. Deterministic tile selection caps visible base probes at 3,000 per frame, and each selected tile owns a deterministic cache slot. Raster shading searches a fixed 5 × 5 neighborhood of that same lattice and blends compatible probes, then falls back to coarse field radiance. The proxy SDF supplies visibility and hit locations, while lighting remains in the two cache layers.

When AABB-SDF GI, global illumination, and `RuntimeContext::gi_gizmos_enabled` are enabled, the renderer draws cache diagnostics directly from GPU storage: surface-probe crosses show sample positions, normal segments are colored by cached probe radiance, and smaller near/far field crosses show each cell's average radiance. This control is independent of the general scene-gizmos setting. The overlay shares the scene depth test and is suppressed during scripted captures that request a clean image. It adds no GPU-to-CPU cache readback.

Rendering turns a list of mesh instances into a perspective image on a supplied window. It owns GPU device, surface, pipeline, buffers, depth target, camera uniform, and presentation. [Windowing](windowing.md) owns the window lifecycle and calls the renderer at event boundaries. Mesh creation belongs to producers such as the engine primitive module or future asset importers.

## State and invariants

Each `MeshInstance` stores a mesh, a `Transform`, and a constant metallic-roughness `Material`. `Renderer` validates the supplied instances, applies transforms to positions and normals, flattens them with per-instance index offsets, and uploads geometry plus material values during initialization. `update_instances` fingerprints each instance's mesh data, transform, material, and probe classification. When nothing changes, it skips vertex generation, GPU writes, shadow invalidation, probe regeneration, and SDF-bound rebuilding. Changed instances rewrite only their vertex ranges; a topology change recreates the vertex, index, and wireframe buffers and refreshes shadow draw ranges and scene bounds. This lets an application replace or resize its scene after renderer initialization while keeping the renderer's pipelines and window resources alive. The assigned `Camera` supplies view, projection, clear-color, and camera-position state; assigned directional lights supply the diffuse and specular lighting array. `update_camera` and `update_lights` write those changes to the uniform and render target. The surface configuration, depth target, and camera aspect match the most recent nonzero window size. The renderer supports one graphics API per instance through `GraphicsApi`; Vulkan is the default. Window mode changes do not change the renderer's API.

The current primitives example contains a white plane, red cube, and green UV sphere generated by the engine primitives module. The separate lighting example adds a room shell, doorway, exterior floor, and enabled irradiance volumes with debug bounds and probe markers. Its `--large-scene` mode adds connected rooms, an exterior colonnade, static props, emissive panels, a warm point light, a focused spot light, and a larger probe layout. Its `--open-world` mode creates a 128×128 terrain with 289 repeated structures, secondary props, emissive landmarks, and a sparse 576-probe volume for performance testing. Its `--gi-test` mode creates a neutral chamber with isolated colored objects and controlled local lights for image-based GI checks. The cube rotates and the sphere moves between two points using the math interpolation helpers. A right-handed perspective camera looks toward them. The WGSL shader combines a low base ambient term with assigned directional, point, and spot lights, three stable texel-snapped directional shadow cascades, spot shadow maps, and nearest-probe indirect irradiance evaluated per vertex and interpolated across each triangle. Depth testing and back-face culling preserve the solid shapes. Rendering is split into explicit directional-shadow cascade, spot-shadow, scene, irradiance-debug, and UI pass methods.

## Public contract

Renderer::capture renders the current scene to an independent target, reads back RGBA8 pixels and depth surfaces at a requested resolution, and restores the visible camera uniform. The returned `CapturedFrame` includes scene depth and the shadow family layers used by the scene's light types. The debug capture coordinator encodes the color image and relevant depth layers as PNG previews.

`Renderer::new` accepts a nonempty slice of `MeshInstance` values and a window surface supported by the selected graphics API. `update_instances` accepts changed transforms and materials, and reallocates flattened geometry buffers when per-instance vertex counts, index data, or total buffer sizes change. It also rebuilds per-instance shadow ranges and scene bounds for that topology change. `update_camera` and `update_lights` rebuild the directional cascade matrices and upload the directional, point, and spot light uniforms. Point and spot lights use range attenuation; spot lights also use inner and outer cone angles. Directional shadows use three practical-split orthographic maps fitted to the active camera frustum with continuous sphere bounds, light-space texel snapping for stable movement, and a 3x3 comparison filter with a center-sample hard core to keep intersecting geometry from producing a lit seam. The optional screen-space contact-shadow pass is disabled by default because directional maps already provide the authoritative shadow; enabling it is an explicit `RenderSettings::contact_shadows_enabled` choice. When enabled, the scene uses a depth prepass and copied depth surface so short contact rays do not conflict with the lit pass depth attachment. `RenderSettings::ambient_intensity` controls the base diffuse fill independently from sky lighting; its default is 0.02, and applications can set it to zero for unfilled lighting tests. `RenderSettings::surface_probes_enabled` controls whether AABB-SDF GI blends screen-driven surface-probe radiance with the coarse world-radiance field. Disabling it leaves GI enabled and shades from the coarse field alone. The probe cache continues updating, so the setting can be toggled without rebuilding the cache. `RenderSettings` can reduce the active cascade count and effective shadow resolution, and controls depth bias, normal bias, and filter radius. `ShadowQuality::{Low, Medium, High, Ultra}` provides stable preset values for application settings and future INI configuration. The application should own parsing and persistence of configuration files and map those values to typed engine settings. The first spot light uses a perspective shadow map. Point lights currently remain direct-only until a cubemap shadow path is added. `update_render_debug_mode` selects lit materials, unlit materials, a wireframe edge pass, direct-light visibility, or a GI-only pass. Irradiance is sampled per fragment from the surface world position. Connected-region labels remain in the CPU visibility grid for probe relocation and bounce filtering. Fragment interpolation blends trilinear support from the receiver-normal-facing side and attenuates near-perpendicular probes by their angular alignment. It renormalizes front-side support before angular attenuation, allowing blends across region boundaries without sampling probes behind the face. It supports authored irradiance volumes and a camera-following clipmap selected through `RuntimeContext::irradiance_mode`; three nested clipmap levels grow in extent as probe density decreases, snap independently to probe-sized steps, and preserve cached values for overlapping world-space probes. Combined mode uses authored volumes first and falls back to the clipmap when they have no usable probes. Irradiance data is read from a storage buffer capped at 4,096 probes across four volume descriptors. The GI-only pass outputs probe irradiance before direct-light evaluation, so shadowed receivers can be checked independently of direct shadowing. `RenderSettings` independently enables directional/spot shadow sampling and irradiance sampling. Disabling irradiance in AABB-SDF mode skips SDF construction, uploads, and GI compute while preserving the cache; re-enabling invalidates and refreshes the field. In authored-probe modes, disabling irradiance stops probe refresh and buffer uploads while retaining cached values. The engine context separately controls whether application debug gizmos are generated and uploaded, allowing large scenes to run without probe, light, and normal overlays. `update_gizmos` uploads world-space debug lines that are depth-tested over the scene. `Mesh` producers use the shared vertex and triangle-index contract; primitive generation is outside the renderer. `Material` currently uses constant base color, metallic, roughness, and emission values; texture-backed properties can be added without changing the mesh contract. `resize` ignores zero dimensions. `render` draws and presents one frame or reports an error.

`GraphicsApi` maps Vulkan, DirectX 12, OpenGL/GLES, and Metal to wgpu backends. Selecting an unavailable API returns an adapter error rather than silently switching APIs.

Experimental AABB-SDF mode uses a camera-centered 64 × 32 × 64 distance/material field built from per-instance axis-aligned bounding boxes. Voxels carry the closest proxy's albedo and emission. The 32 × 16 × 32 m SDF uses 0.5 m voxels and a toroidal texture offset. Its vertical origin is biased down so 87.5% of the volume lies below the camera. A camera shift rebuilds only newly exposed slabs; full rebuilds query a BVH over the proxy bounds and process voxel rows across available CPU threads. Geometry changes and shifts larger than the SDF still cause a full rebuild. Voxels upload by dirty texture regions. The two camera-centered fallback radiance levels each use 8 × 4 × 8 cells and six directional RGB lobes per cell: the near level covers 16 × 8 × 16 m at 2 m spacing, while the far level covers 32 × 16 × 32 m at 4 m spacing; 87.5% of each vertical range lies below the camera. A compute pass updates both levels, tracing SDF rays at initialization and mode changes, for newly exposed cells, and for a rotating one-thirty-second of retained cells during a 32-frame refresh window after geometry, light, or sky changes. Retained cells stop refreshing when that window ends. Toroidal indexing preserves radiance for overlapping world cells during camera translation. The full-resolution scene pass samples the field only where screen-probe coverage is incomplete, trilinearly evaluates the six directional lobes, rejects grid cells embedded in SDF solids, and normalizes interpolation by visibility-weighted support. It samples the near field for pixels inside its volume and samples the far field only outside the near volume or inside its blend band. These coarse field cells provide low-frequency fallback lighting; the screen-driven surface cache supplies local GI where it has support. Surface-probe anchors remain at the world points associated with their stored radiance; a move greater than one quarter SDF voxel triggers immediate resampling, while smaller movement lowers the old sample's confidence. Their stable-slot radiance blends are 0.65 for base probes and up to 0.9 for prioritized probes, with moved anchors replaced immediately. Their combined radiance storage is about 48 KiB. Concave or detailed meshes can become solid boxes and falsely occlude secondary rays, and the coarse SDF still adds intersection error; the 0.3 m room-test walls remain thinner than one SDF voxel. Six directions intentionally trade angular detail for a smaller fallback cache. `RendererPerformanceStats` separates CPU SDF build time, upload preparation/enqueue time, rebuilt voxel count, and GPU radiance-field update time. GPU timings are updated when the debug-capture timestamp report is read.

## Lifecycle

The renderer creates its GPU resources during window resume and drops them on suspension. During redraw, stable instance data is a no-op; changed instances update only their vertex ranges, while topology changes replace the geometry buffers and related shadow-range metadata. On resize it reconfigures the surface, recreates full-resolution scene depth, and updates camera projection. The fixed world-space SDF and radiance-field resources do not depend on display resolution. On an outdated surface it reconfigures; on a lost surface it recreates the surface; on timeout or occlusion it skips a frame.

## Verification and open questions

`cargo test --workspace --offline` and `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --offline -- -D warnings` passed on Windows. The geometry test checks outward triangle winding. Windowed, borderless, and exclusive-request runs initialized Vulkan and presented a first frame. DirectX 12 also presented a first frame. Pixels, OpenGL, Metal, native exclusive mode, and non-Windows platforms have not been directly inspected in this environment.
RenderSettings::secondary_light_shadowing applies the primary directional visibility as a temporary shared occlusion fallback to secondary direct lights. This prevents unshadowed fill and local lights from washing out primary shadows while per-light shadow resources are being unified.
The camera uniform now carries a per-light shadow mode: directional, spot, or none. This removes light-index assumptions and is the contract used to attach future point cubemap resources.
ShadowBudget and ShadowScheduler now provide the per-frame policy layer for dynamic shadow updates. They rank directional, spot, and point lights separately so atlas and cubemap resources can be scheduled without changing light evaluation.
Shadow requests now include a ShadowResourceKind and resource slot, separating light scheduling from future directional cascade, spot atlas, and point cubemap allocation.
ShadowResourceTable now rebuilds from scheduled requests and records stable resource kind/slot/resolution allocations. GPU atlas and cubemap allocation can consume this table without changing scheduler state.
ShadowResourceAllocation now includes atlas layer and offset coordinates. Spot resources are laid out in a two-column atlas grid; directional cascades and point faces reserve independent layers.
Renderer::shadow_requests and Renderer::shadow_resource_allocations expose the scheduler/resource state for editor diagnostics and performance panels.
ShadowBudget now includes atlas_size. Spot allocations are rejected when their grid cell cannot fit, allowing the scheduler/resource layer to enforce atlas capacity before GPU allocation.
ShadowResourceAllocation now carries the owning light_index and Renderer exposes lookup by light index. This makes atlas/cubemap mappings deterministic for future GPU shadow passes. Atlas capacity is covered by a renderer test; spot cells beyond the configured atlas are rejected.
ShadowScheduler now enforces spot atlas capacity using the configured atlas_size and SPOT_SHADOW_MAP_SIZE before emitting requests. This prevents scheduled spot lights from lacking an allocation; coverage is provided by a scheduler test.
Spot shadow rendering now targets atlas regions: camera data carries per-slot matrices and UV rectangles, the atlas texture is sized from ShadowBudget::atlas_size, and scheduled spot passes render with viewport/scissor into their assigned cells. The directional and point GPU paths remain separate; point cubemap rendering is still pending.
All atlas-capable spot lights now receive their own shader shadow mode and slot metadata; the first multi-spot slot mapping is covered by a renderer test.
PointCube allocations now reserve six consecutive array layers and expose point_face_layer for the upcoming cubemap shadow pass. Directional and spot atlas layer assignments remain unchanged.
Added deterministic point_shadow_view_projections for the six cubemap faces, with fixed directions/up vectors and a 90-degree projection. The matrix basis is covered by a renderer test; GPU face rendering remains next.
Point shadow GPU groundwork is now active: the renderer allocates a six-layer-per-point depth array and renders each scheduled point light into six face views using point_shadow_view_projections. Point shadows are not sampled by the scene shader yet, so this pass is preparatory and should be profiled before enabling broader budgets.
Gizmo rendering now binds the depth bind group required by the shared pipeline layout. Capture paths use their temporary depth bind group; normal frames use the renderer depth bind group, preventing validation failures when gizmos are enabled.
Point-light shadow sampling now uses the six-face depth array: the shader selects a cubemap face from the light-to-surface vector, projects through the matching matrix, and compares the corresponding depth layer. The room test disables irradiance probes to isolate direct point-shadow occlusion.
Ambient occlusion is now a separate RenderSettings feature from direct shadows and irradiance. It uses a small scene-depth neighborhood query in the scene shader, is independently scriptable with capture.set_ambient_occlusion(bool), and is covered by the room-light-bleed capture.
Overhead room captures exposed overextended point-shadow wedges at wall corners. Point shadow depth bias was adjusted from -0.0008 to -0.0025 to reduce false self-occlusion while retaining wall blocking; overhead captures were regenerated for validation.
Point-light depth comparison now uses a positive receiver bias (0.0008), matching the renderer's LessEqual comparison convention. Overhead and room captures show direct shadows extending from inner walls toward the outer floor instead of leaking illumination through them.
Fixed point/spot shadow wall bleed by disabling face culling in the local-light depth pipeline. Front-face culling recorded far faces of closed wall boxes, allowing light through the near wall; rendering both faces captures the nearest occluder.
Local-light depth rendering now culls back faces and uses a small negative point receiver bias. This captures the near wall surface while avoiding self-shadowing on walls facing the point light; room captures show the full central floor lit with exterior wall shadows.
Added ShadowVisibility render debug mode. It outputs accumulated direct-light visibility as grayscale, independent of material color and GI, and can be selected from capture scripts with set_render_mode("shadow_visibility"). Room captures confirm visible and wall-occluded regions.
Disabled the transitional secondary_light_shadowing fallback by default. Direct-light visibility is now evaluated per light; secondary lights no longer inherit primary shadow occlusion unless explicitly enabled.
Added direct_light_visibility_enabled to isolate direct-light evaluation from shadow visibility. Disabling it forces direct lights visible; enabling it multiplies each light by its own shadow result.
Point shadow capture and rendering now use one uniform buffer per cube face. This preserves each face's view-projection matrix within a single command submission; a previous shared-buffer path caused all six faces to contain the final face projection.
Probe irradiance now checks local point and spot light visibility through the visibility grid before adding their direct contribution to a probe. This keeps indirect light from being injected through enclosed walls while preserving directional sky lighting.
Probe surface gathering uses a reduced contribution scale and stronger distance falloff so dense wall samples do not over-brighten enclosed walls or wash out the spatial falloff across a room.

The lighting inspector exposes a master gizmo switch and independent mesh-normal, light, global-GI, and screen-surface-probe toggles. Its `Freeze GI radiance` diagnostic option keeps existing screen-probe anchors and radiance fixed, pauses coarse world-field refresh, and still initializes newly created screen probes. The renderer can display surface-probe and coarse-radiance-field markers independently. `Selected GI cell rays` draws only the six directions belonging to a chosen near or far field cell. Ray-segment colors identify geometry status (green hit, orange miss, red invalid result); endpoint-marker rings identify lighting status, termination reason, and last sampled SDF distance. Segment length represents total ray travel. Hit markers include normal arrows, and a cell outline identifies the selected cell. The inspector shows a legend for these signals. `SDF occupied voxels` draws translucent boxes for boundary voxels with negative signed distance. Both diagnostics share the scene depth test and are suppressed during clean scripted captures. Button labels are centered inside their controls.
